#1 · Security Model · OpenAINov 5Benchmarks vendor-reported — unverified

OpenAI releases GPT-6 Cyber, a security model built to find and fix exploits

OpenAI launched GPT-6 Cyber, a specialized model for authorized vulnerability investigation, reproduction, and fix verification. It scored 48.5% on ExploitGym — turning known vulnerabilities into working exploits — versus 32.1% for GPT-5.6 Cyber and 42.4% for flagship GPT-6 Astra, a roughly 51% relative gain. OpenAI says researchers used it to develop 948 privilege-escalation exploits in Linux and to find 81 previously unknown high-severity Firefox vulnerabilities, which Mozilla has fixed. The model is classified “Critical” in cybersecurity under OpenAI's Preparedness Framework, and OpenAI says it stays within authorized scope 99% of the time (up from 95%). Access is initially limited to select customers approved for OpenAI's Daybreak Red program, via the API and Codex, expanding to all customers in the coming weeks.

Key players: OpenAI, Mozilla, Fouad Matin (OpenAI).

#5 · Open Source · KakaoNov 6Single-sourced · Speed claims vendor-reported

Kakao open-sources Kanana-2-v-decide, a model that outputs decisions instead of text

Kakao open-sourced Kanana-2-v-decide, an unusual multimodal model that does not generate text at all — it outputs decisions (probabilities, selections, scores). Built on Kakao's Kanana-2-v-3b with an embedding-based architecture that reuses vector representations of choices, it averages under 0.04 seconds per response on a single NVIDIA H200 GPU — claimed to be roughly 43× faster than OpenAI's GPT-6 Luna. The design points to a new class of ultra-low-latency decision models for real-time systems, trading generative flexibility for speed.

Key players: Kakao.

Google announced it will build its first Wyoming data center — a long-term, $17 billion investment in Laramie County (Cheyenne area, “Project Tembo”) with partners Tallgrass Energy, Black Hills Energy, American Electric Power, and Related Digital. #21 · Personal Finance · Anthropic / PlaidNov 5

Anthropic launches Personal Finance in Claude — read-only bank linking via Plaid (US beta)

Anthropic launched Personal Finance in Claude, a beta that lets US subscribers on Pro ($20/mo), Max, and Ultra plans link bank, card, loan, and investment accounts through Plaid and ask Claude questions about their own money in plain language — spending, bills, savings, cash flow. Access is strictly read-only: Claude cannot move, manage, or invest money, and the feature is not designed to give investment advice. It reads balances, credit limits, and up to 24 months of transactions for up to 16 linked banks, and chats in the Personal Finance hub are not used for model training. The launch follows OpenAI's account-connected finance tools via Plaid in May and marks AI assistants' push into personal finance — with the privacy stakes that come with it.

Key players: Anthropic, Plaid.

06 / POLICY & SAFETY

Data-center backlash becomes the story

#27 · Policy · OpenAINov 5

OpenAI publishes National Community Compact and Compute Campus Policy Blueprint

OpenAI published a National Community Compact and a Compute Campus Policy Blueprint, pledging that at future campuses it owns and operates it will pay the full cost of electricity and grid infrastructure (with financial assurances before major spending), publish construction plans and independent noise studies, avoid NDAs with public officials, and give each campus its own local compact shaped by residents, with an annual public report. The blueprint asks lawmakers to make similar rules apply industry-wide. It builds on the Grid Protectors Program (Nov 2), under which Georgia Power can cut up to 1,000 MW at OpenAI's planned Effingham County, Georgia campus during peak demand. Notably, the compact does not cover partner-built sites such as Oracle's, which supply much of OpenAI's compute.

Key players: OpenAI, Georgia Power.

#28 · Politics · U.S. ElectionsNov 3 election · Reported Nov 5–6

Voters back data-center limits in 40 of 41 ballot measures; 57% say AI will do more harm than good

In the November 3 US elections, voters backed limits on data-center development in 40 of 41 local ballot measures (33 restrictions passed by the morning after, with more projected), and 7 pro-development plans failed. Exit polls found 57% of voters believe AI will do more harm than good for society. The backlash is already reshaping AI infrastructure politics: it prompted OpenAI's community compact, Google's community commitments in Wyoming, and calls from Democratic lawmakers (including Texas governor-elect Gina Hinojosa) to curb data-center expansion. It is the clearest political headwind yet for the AI buildout.

Key players: U.S. voters; OpenAI; Google; Gina Hinojosa.

#29 · Safety · OpenAINov 5

OpenAI's GPT-6 Cyber gated behind Daybreak Red approval — a safety-gating precedent

OpenAI is limiting GPT-6 Cyber — its most capable offensive-security model, rated “Critical” under its Preparedness Framework — to customers approved for its Daybreak Red program for authorized security work, rather than releasing it broadly. The gated rollout is a notable test of whether frontier labs can ship dual-use capabilities responsibly: the same model that finds real vulnerabilities could be misused outside authorized testing, and OpenAI is betting access controls plus scope-adherence training (99% in-bounds) are the answer.

Perplexity releases BrowseSafe++ benchmark against prompt injection

The Perplexity Secure Intelligence Institute released BrowseSafe++, a successor to its BrowseSafe benchmark (December 2025) — an open benchmark dedicated to indirect prompt injection targeting agents that browse the web — along with BrowseSafe-Guard detectors.
