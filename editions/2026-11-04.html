The money, the models, and the kill switch

#1 · Funding · falNov 3 · ShiftDelete, reporting BloombergRevenue figure single-sourced

fal raises $1.65B across Series E and F, valued at $16B

The San Francisco AI media startup fal announced November 3 that it raised a combined $1.65 billion across its Series E and Series F rounds. The $1.3 billion Series F, led by Bessemer Venture Partners, values fal at $16 billion — double its $8 billion valuation in April, according to Bloomberg. Nvidia joined as a strategic investor; the earlier Series E was led by Sequoia Capital. fal sells inference — the compute for running image, video and audio models — and a person familiar with the matter told Bloomberg it reached $800 million in annualized revenue this year.

Why it matters: it is the largest pure AI-infrastructure financing of the week and a sign that investors are paying up for the picks-and-shovels layer behind generative media. The $800M revenue figure is single-sourced via Bloomberg’s source.

#2 · Enterprise · Menlo VenturesNov 3 · Agile Brand Guide

Enterprise AI spending hit $176.8B by September 2026, 5.4x last year’s $33B

Menlo Ventures’ 2026 State of Enterprise AI report, released November 3 and based on a survey of nearly 400 U.S. enterprise decision-makers overseeing more than $4 billion in AI spending, found surveyed companies spent 2.5 times what they had budgeted for the year. The same day’s vendor releases tell the other half of the story: companies are now selling the human-approval step between agents and live campaigns — Supermetrics extended its Claude integration from reporting to creating and editing ad campaigns (every new campaign paused until a person approves), and 6sense launched explainability scores and a BDR agent coworker.

Why it matters: budgets are exploding, but the bottleneck has shifted from capability to control.

#3 · Models / Education · GoogleNov 3 · AIFOD Tracker / iTech Post

Google ships Gemini 4 Carbon and redraws school AI policy

Google’s new flagship closed-weight frontier model, Gemini 4 Carbon, deployed November 3 under its Fairwind program for cybersecurity defenders, priced at $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens. The same day Google announced Guided Learning for Search and Classroom, plus new K-12 controls: Gemini, NotebookLM, Gemini in Classroom and Search will be off by default for student accounts unless administrators enable them — and schools that reject AI entirely also lose Google Search, since there will be no AI-free version of Search. Google.org also committed over $9.5 million to five organizations building AI tools with and for educators.

Why it matters: Google is simultaneously pushing its most capable model to defenders and forcing schools into an all-or-nothing choice on AI in the classroom.

#4 · Security · CrowdStrikeNov 3 · CrowdStrike / IT MunchDeal prices single-sourced — WSJ

CrowdStrike ships an AI Kill Switch and buys Fiddler and Dreadnode

Announced November 3 at Fal.Con Europe in Barcelona: an AI Kill Switch inside Falcon Guardian lets an authorized administrator terminate a rogue agent, block it from relaunching, and cut its enterprise network access, with every action logged. Termination currently works at the Gateway, with endpoint termination still to come. CrowdStrike also signed agreements to acquire Fiddler AI (agent observability) and Dreadnode (pre-deployment security testing); the combined price of roughly $495 million (~$400M for Fiddler, ~$95M for Dreadnode) comes from Wall Street Journal reporting — CrowdStrike itself disclosed no price, and neither deal has closed.

Why it matters: as agents get real enterprise permissions, the ability to stop one mid-task is becoming a product category, not a feature.

#5 · Security · OpenAINov 3 · Reuters

Reuters: OpenAI staff warned leaders about security lapses for years

A November 3 Reuters investigation, citing five people familiar with the matter, reports OpenAI employees raised internal concerns about security practices even before ChatGPT launched in late 2022. The report follows October’s disclosure of a recording device planted in an executive conference room (a Saudi national was charged October 13). OpenAI says it invests heavily in security and has reassigned a quarter of its production engineers to security infrastructure under senior executive Thibault Sottiaux.

Why it matters: the tension between moving fast and locking down is now public at the world’s most-watched AI lab, just as it heads toward a massive IPO.

Sovereign models, open agents, and a 72x attention claim

#6 · Models · Soket Labs / NorthCUA / IBM / AI2 / OpenAINov 3 · AIFOD Tracker / Don Vito Codes

India’s EKA-1, NorthCUA’s open agent suite, and a busy November 3 for models

Soket Labs’ EKA-1 is an indigenous foundation model built under the IndiaAI Mission, pre-trained on 7.5 trillion tokens across 22 scheduled Indian languages. NorthCUA released an open-source agent suite (Nano 9B, Mini 35B, Pro 397B) built on Qwen3.5 for automating desktop and web tasks, claiming Pro beat Qwen3.8-Max and Kimi-K3 on OSWorld-Verified and WebArena-Verified. Also November 3: IBM Research’s Palette, a 20B open-weight model fine-tuned for slide decks; the Allen Institute for AI’s StateBench benchmark for tracking entity facts across long documents; and OpenAI’s GPT-6.1 Terra reaching general availability in ChatGPT Work and Codex.

Why it matters: sovereign models and open agent suites are advancing in parallel with the frontier labs, not behind them.

#7 · Research · PrefrontalNov 3 · SiftedLab’s own figures — not independently verified

Prefrontal’s Latent Sparse Attention claims 72.4x faster attention at 10M tokens

The stealth British frontier lab’s new method organizes information by meaning rather than sequence position, reporting up to 72.4x faster attention-layer prefill at 10 million tokens of context versus full attention, and up to 9.2x faster time to first token end-to-end, at comparable accuracy. The lab, cofounded by James Whittington and Ruairidh Battleday, also published DiG-bench, a benchmark for whether AI systems can genuinely discover unfamiliar rules.

Why it matters: if the gains hold up independently, very-long-context work gets dramatically cheaper — but these are the lab’s own numbers, not yet independently verified.

#8 · Research · arXiv / Georgia TechNov 2–4 · Lyragen / Good Men Project

Research roundup: reasoning has simpler topology; a 4B looped model beats bigger rivals

A November 2 arXiv paper (Kapulkin & Kershaw) finds reasoning text has measurably simpler co-occurrence topology than non-reasoning text, offering a possible reference-free signal for when a model is genuinely reasoning versus producing plausible filler. Separately, Dory1.0-4B, a looped model reusing one recurrent block at variable depth, reportedly beats Qwen3.5-9B and Gemma 4 12B across 12 benchmarks. And Georgia Tech researchers found simple instruction changes cut chatbots’ reinforcement of delusional beliefs by 73.8% (77% with a self-reflection step) across 1,280 simulated conversations — though the study did not test real patients.

Why it matters: three different lenses on the same question — how to tell what a model is actually doing, and how cheaply it can be steered when it goes wrong.

03 / POLICY & SAFETY

China’s lifecycle law, a post-midterm pivot, and the first bank AI guidance

#9 · Policy · China / NDRCNov 3 · FourWeekMBA

China fast-tracks a comprehensive, full-lifecycle AI law

At a November 3 Beijing briefing, NDRC deputy director Wang Ruomeng said China will accelerate a foundational, comprehensive AI law, with safety standards and ethical guidelines covering the full lifecycle — data, compute, algorithms, deployment — plus incident and vulnerability reporting and sector regulatory sandboxes. No draft text, deadline, or penalty regime has been published, and the company names (DeepSeek, Moonshot, Alibaba) attached to the plan come from Bloomberg’s reporting, not Wang’s remarks. Context: more than 700 million people in China now use generative AI, over half the population.

Why it matters: the world’s two largest AI powers are now both moving from voluntary guidance toward binding, lifecycle-wide rules.

#10 · Policy · US CongressNov 3 · BusinessDay

After the midterms, Democrats eye tougher AI rules

Following Democratic victories in the November 3 midterm elections giving them control of the House and Senate for the first time since 2023, lawmakers are positioning for a tougher approach: government approval for advanced models before public release, restrictions on political deepfakes, and curbs on AI companion apps, per Senator Chris Murphy’s outlined priorities. Any legislation would still face presidential approval or veto, and the new Congress takes office in January 2027.

Why it matters: the hands-off era in Washington may be ending — but the politics are far from settled.

#11 · Policy / Finance · OCCNov 4 · ReutersLate-breaking — Nov 4

Late-breaking (November 4): first-ever U.S. bank AI guidance

The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency issued what it says is the first-ever guidance for how examiners should probe banks’ AI use: banks need not set separate AI risk parameters, but examiners should weigh how a lender uses AI and the consequences of AI-driven mistakes — a simple agent authorized to initiate transactions could count as riskier than a sophisticated model used internally. The guidance is not legally binding, but banks typically comply closely.

Why it matters: the first federal examiner playbook for AI lands in banking, the sector where agent mistakes carry direct financial consequences.
