#1 · Microsoft · CopilotSeptember 25, 2026

Microsoft turns Copilot into an agentic work platform

Microsoft unveiled Code, a natural-language builder for apps and dashboards powered by GitHub Copilot technology; Autopilot, a private-preview successor to June’s Scout agent that works continuously with its own directory identity and controllable permissions; and Word, Excel and PowerPoint embedded directly inside Copilot.

Early-access customers are due to get Code at the end of September, while Microsoft 365 Premium and Pro subscribers can preview it later this year. New cost-management controls will also let employees see AI usage costs as enterprises watch for runaway spending.

Why it matters

Microsoft is racing ChatGPT and Claude by making Copilot a one-stop office-work system rather than another chatbot. Its emphasis on security, compliance and governance targets the biggest enterprise adoption blocker.

#2 · U.S.–U.K. · Model testingSeptember 24, 2026Single-sourced

White House asked OpenAI and Anthropic to delay U.K. AISI access

Politico reported that the Office of the National Cyber Director asked the two U.S. labs not to share new frontier models with the U.K.’s AI Safety Institute until the U.S. government had tested them first. A senior administration official described that as standing policy “with every new frontier model that comes out.”

Anthropic’s launch language said Claude Mythos 5.1 was available only to a set of U.S. organizations. OpenAI did not comment and reportedly gave the U.K. pre-release GPT-6 Astra access, suggesting uneven application. Caveat: this account relies on a Politico exclusive; the linked analysis summarizes that reporting.

Why it matters

Access for an allied safety agency now appears to depend partly on how individual companies interpret a White House request, complicating U.S.–U.K. coordination.

#3 · Anthropic · Life sciencesSeptember 25, 2026Architecture only

Claude identifies a previously unknown CRISPR-like biological system

In the first work from Anthropic’s new life sciences laboratory, Claude identified array-associated reverse transcriptases (ART) in bacteriophage DNA: a reverse-transcriptase enzyme, a partner gene and a long array of repeating DNA sequences. Human researchers then validated the system.

Caveat: “CRISPR-like” describes the system’s architecture. There is no evidence yet that ART can be programmed to edit DNA—the defining capability of CRISPR.

Why it matters

This is AI doing more than analyzing a supplied dataset: it flagged a genuinely novel biological system for researchers to test.

#4 · OpenAI · Google · AnthropicSeptember 24, 2026Single-sourced

Frontier labs plan a self-regulatory AI standards body

OpenAI, Google and Anthropic reportedly aim to launch a standards body by the end of 2026 or early 2027. It would support third-party pre-deployment testing, set incident-reporting practices, define voluntary safety commitments and qualify independent auditors—without government oversight.

Critics worry the effort could box out open-source and smaller developers. It would also overlap with the Frontier Model Forum the same labs helped establish in 2023. Related reporting said Dario Amodei and Sam Altman told a September 24 U.N. Security Council session that global standards are needed. Caveat: the plan comes from The Information citing unnamed sources.

Why it matters

The frontier labs are trying to shape the rules before governments do.

Agent sprawl creates a market for governance

#1 · Dataiku · Agent managementSeptember 24–25, 2026Company release

Dataiku launches a control plane for enterprise AI agents

Announced at Dataiku’s Succeed conference in New York, the standalone Agent Management product inventories agents across AWS Bedrock, Databricks Agents, Google Vertex, Microsoft Copilot Studio and Azure Foundry, Salesforce Agentforce and Snowflake Cortex. OpenTelemetry covers custom setups.

It measures business and technical performance, flags high-risk agents, and is scheduled for general availability in October 2026 with per-instance pricing and per-agent monitoring metering. Dataiku cites IBM research saying fewer than one in five organizations maintains a complete AI-system inventory.

Why it matters

As agent sprawl grows, inventory, risk scoring and cross-platform oversight are becoming a product category of their own.

05 / RESEARCH

AI moves from biological prediction toward design

#1 · Phage design · Cell SystemsSeptember 24, 2026Dual-use

AI-guided mutations help bacteriophages infect bacteria up to one million times more effectively

Researchers trained a model on tens of thousands of bacteriophage mutations and generated new variants that sharply increased infection effectiveness. The work maps which amino acids help phages recognize host sugar coatings and break through, with potential applications in phage therapy for urinary-tract infections, microbiome-linked diseases and agricultural pathogens such as Salmonella.

The paper is Naia Novy et al., Cell Systems (2026), DOI 10.1016/j.cels.2026.101712. Caveat: the same capability has obvious dual-use implications.

Why it matters

AI-designed biological systems are crossing from prediction into experimentally useful design.

06 / POLICY & SAFETY

Governments turn broad AI laws into operating deadlines

#1 · New York · RAISE ActSeptember 23, 2026

New York’s RAISE Act gets a compliance calendar

Large frontier developers must register beginning in November 2026. Public safety protocols, regular reporting and 72-hour critical-incident reporting take effect in January 2027. Governor Kathy Hochul announced the schedule on September 21 and appointed Marc Gilman to lead the new Office of Digital Innovation, Governance, Integrity and Trust within the Department of Financial Services.

Covered developers must publish safety frameworks, file quarterly catastrophic-risk assessments and register with the state.

Why it matters

Banks and fintechs that depend on frontier models now have concrete vendor-management deadlines—and a provider incident could force service changes in the middle of a contract.
