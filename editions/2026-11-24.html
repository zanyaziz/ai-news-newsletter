01 / COMPANY & LEADERSHIP

The fastest CEO reversal in tech history

#1 · Company / Leadership · NvidiaMon, Nov 23, 2026

Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO

Nvidia’s board unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as president, CEO and director, effective immediately — just 11 days after he resigned amid revelations of extramarital affairs, including one with a woman U.S. officials suspect of working for Chinese intelligence. The board said it was satisfied with Huang’s candor and, after input from senior U.S. government officials, is confident his return poses no national security risk.

Shares surged roughly 30–34%, adding nearly $1 trillion in market value; the reversal followed pressure from President Trump and other administration officials. Why it matters: the episode puts the governance of the AI era’s most systemically important company — and the role of government pressure in a board’s decision — under a spotlight that an ongoing independent committee review will keep lit. Interim CEO Colette Kress returns to the CFO role.

Key people/companies: Jensen Huang; Colette Kress; Nvidia board; President Trump.

02 / POLICY & REGULATION

States take Grok to court

#2 · Policy / Regulation / Safety · SpaceX / xAIMon, Nov 23, 2026

Virginia and Oregon sue SpaceX over Grok generating child sexual abuse imagery

The attorneys general of Virginia (Jay Jones) and Oregon (Dan Rayfield) filed the first state lawsuits against Elon Musk’s SpaceX Super Intelligence Corp — parent of X, xAI and Grok — alleging Grok’s image-generation “spicy mode” let users turn ordinary photos into sexualized images and videos, including of children, while the company misled consumers about its safeguards.

The suits cite a Center for Countering Digital Hate study estimating Grok generated ~3 million sexualized images on X between Dec 29, 2025 and Jan 8, 2026, including ~23,000 appearing to depict children. The states seek civil penalties (up to $2,500 per violation in Virginia), injunctions, restitution and disgorgement of profits. Why it matters: the first state-level legal test of a generator’s liability for sexualized imagery of real people — and children — with remedies that could force product changes well beyond two states.

Key people/companies: Jay Jones (Virginia AG); Dan Rayfield (Oregon AG); Elon Musk; SpaceX Super Intelligence Corp; X; xAI; Grok; Center for Countering Digital Hate.

03 / RESEARCH BREAKTHROUGHS

Machines write the proofs now

#3 · Research · Anthropic / OpenAIMon, Nov 23, 2026AI-discovered — neither proof human-checked

Anthropic and OpenAI post competing VP ≠ VNP proofs — both AI-discovered, neither human-checked

Anthropic posted a 70-page paper credited solely to an internal Claude research model claiming a proof of VP ≠ VNP — Valiant’s 1979 conjecture, the algebraic analogue of P vs NP — with a Lean 4 formalization spanning 324 files. Anthropic states plainly that no human mathematician has checked the proof; Lean verifies internal logical consistency, not whether the formal statements match the English claims.

Hours later OpenAI posted its own 30-page proof in its public math repo, saying its model obtained the result on Nov 6. Why it matters: two frontier labs claiming the same landmark result within hours is a new kind of scientific race — but both proofs are machine-checked and unverified by humans, and neither settles Boolean P vs NP.

Key people/companies: Anthropic (Claude research model); OpenAI; Lean 4.

#4 · Research · AnthropicReleased Fri, Nov 20 · Verified Sat, Nov 21 · Coverage Mon, Nov 23, 2026Release outside 48-hour window — see note

Claude-assisted proof of the Riemann Hypothesis — verification lands, coverage peaks

Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge released a formal Lean proof of the Riemann Hypothesis (and the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis), developed with Claude over ~six weeks, as ~17 million lines across 175,187 Lean files. On Nov 21 the Lean Focused Research Organization and Mathlib Initiative announced independent verification with “full confidence” the proof is correct.

The proof itself is not yet understood by humans — no natural-language version exists yet — and the Clay Mathematics Institute still lists the hypothesis as unsolved. Caveat: the underlying release is Nov 20, outside the strict 48-hour window; it is included because verification and mainstream coverage landed Nov 21–23.

Key people/companies: Levent Alpöge; Anthropic (Claude); Lean Focused Research Organization; Mathlib Initiative; Clay Mathematics Institute.

#5 · Research · Google DeepMindMon, Nov 23, 2026Estimates — not run on quantum hardware

Google DeepMind: Gemini agent teams find formally verified quantum algorithms, up to 290× cheaper

Google DeepMind and Google Quantum AI used teams of Gemini 4 agents to design quantum circuits and formally verify them in the Lean 4 theorem prover, reporting a 290× cost reduction for a quantum arcsin routine and 2.5–12× improvements for simulations relevant to fusion energy, photochemistry and drug metabolism — for example, fusion memory cut from 5,650 to 4,338 error-corrected qubits. The team open-sourced its Lean library, problem specifications and verified solutions on GitHub.

Why it matters: verified-by-construction quantum algorithms could shrink the hardware bar for useful quantum computing — if the estimates hold. Caveat: the figures are resource estimates against author-constructed baselines for future error-corrected machines, not runs on quantum hardware.

Key people/companies: Google DeepMind; Google Quantum AI; Gemini 4; Lean 4.

04 / MODEL RELEASES

Looping layers, no benchmarks

#6 · Model Release · Z.ai (Zhipu AI)Mon, Nov 23, 2026No benchmark table — capability claim unverified

Z.ai launches GLM-5.4, a 750B “Loop Transformer” flagship

Chinese lab Z.ai (Zhipu AI) launched GLM-5.4, its first flagship built on a Loop Transformer: it runs the same 28 layers of the ~750B-parameter GLM-5 base twice per forward pass, which the company says reaches intelligence levels comparable to 2T–3T-parameter models without adding parameters. It is available now via the GLM Coding Plan, ZCode and Z.ai Chat, with vision support and 50% more weekly quota than GLM-5.3; API access and open weights are promised after safety review, with no date.

Why it matters: if looping depth really substitutes for raw scale, it changes the cost math for frontier-class models. Caveat: Z.ai published no benchmark table for GLM-5.4, so the headline capability claim is unverified; the only quantitative evidence is an ablation on a 30B model.

Key people/companies: Z.ai (Zhipu AI).

05 / COMPANY & DEALS

Amazon buys speed for Trainium

#7 · Company / M&A · Amazon / DecartMon, Nov 23, 2026~$7.5B price Bloomberg-reported, not confirmed

Amazon acquires Decart’s AI optimization tech in reported ~$7.5B deal to boost Trainium

Amazon agreed to acquire the technology behind Israeli startup Decart’s Optimization Stack (DOS) — software that helps AI models run efficiently across Nvidia, Google and Amazon hardware — plus Decart’s three co-founders and research team. The deal is aimed at squeezing more performance from Amazon’s Trainium chips as it prepares to sell them to outside customers as a Nvidia competitor.

Amazon did not disclose terms; Bloomberg reported ~$7.5B. It is an asset purchase, not a full acquisition — Decart’s world models (e.g. Oasis) are not included. Why it matters: Amazon is buying the software layer that makes non-Nvidia silicon competitive, a direct bet that optimization — not just chips — decides who can challenge Nvidia.

Key people/companies: Amazon (Trainium); Decart; Nvidia; Google.

06 / PRODUCT LAUNCHES

Agents ship code, guard servers, read your wallet

#8 · Product Launch · AnthropicMon, Nov 23, 2026

Anthropic launches Claude Ship: AI agents that review, fix and approve pull requests

Anthropic launched Claude Ship, a suite of three agents for GitHub-hosted Team and Enterprise customers: Code Review (generally available since October), PR Steward (beta — pushes fixes for failing checks, conflicts and review feedback) and Claude Approvals (beta — applies a team-written approval policy file, like a smarter CODEOWNERS). A new /ship command in Claude Code can push a branch, open a PR, review it and watch it for up to seven days.

Anthropic says its own Claude Code team more than doubled merged PRs June–September with 40%+ fewer reversions. Why it matters: Ship moves agents from writing code to owning the whole merge pipeline — review, repair and approval included. Eligible Enterprise orgs can claim at least $10,000 in credits by Jan 15, 2027.

Key people/companies: Anthropic; Claude Code; GitHub.

#10 · Product Launch / Security · OpenAI / CiscoMon, Nov 23, 2026

OpenAI and Cisco launch Shirudo, open-source AI runtime shields for unpatched Linux

OpenAI and Cisco launched Shirudo (Japanese for “shield”), an open-source Linux runtime that lets AI agents write, test and deploy temporary eBPF-based “shields” blocking exploits on live systems without reboots while teams prepare real patches. Shields are cryptographically signed, TPM-verified, and retire when the patch lands. In a demo, a Codex-generated shield blocked a Java4Shell exploit against a running Elasticsearch service without interruption.

Built as an agent-centric rewrite of Cisco/Isovalent’s Tetragon and BSD 2-Clause licensed, Shirudo turns the patching gap — the window attackers exploit most — into an agent task. A technical webinar is set for Dec 15. Why it matters: runtime self-defense by agents, shipped as open source by two of the biggest names in AI and networking.

Key people/companies: OpenAI (Codex); Cisco; Isovalent (Tetragon).
