01 / INFRASTRUCTURE & RELIABILITY

One database, three and a half hours down

#1 · Infrastructure · OpenAISat, Nov 21, 2026 · 1:48–5:21 p.m. PT

OpenAI suffers 3.5-hour outage — 75% of ChatGPT and API requests fail

OpenAI suffered a 211-minute outage on Saturday afternoon after scheduled maintenance on a Postgres database service used by its Primary API service caused both read replicas to fail simultaneously. Because that service handles authentication across the whole stack, failure rates peaked at 75% across paid and free ChatGPT and the API — Chat Completions, Fine-tuning, Embeddings, Images, Batch, Audio, Moderations, Realtime and Files, plus ChatGPT login, conversations, search, file uploads and voice mode.

OpenAI restored service with replacement replicas, placed a moratorium on similar maintenance, retuned connection settings, and shipped a feature flag to shift traffic off replicas by percentage. All systems were operational by Sunday morning. Why it matters: a sharp reminder of how fragile single-provider agent stacks are — and it landed a day after the Nasdaq’s worst week since 2020.

Key people/companies: OpenAI; ChatGPT.

02 / RESEARCH & SCIENCE

A claimed proof, and a sobering benchmark

#2 · Research · Anthropic / ClaudeClaim Fri, Nov 20 · Verified Sat, Nov 21, 2026Clay Institute still lists problem as unsolved

Claude-assisted proof of the Riemann Hypothesis claimed — independently verified in Lean

Levent Alpöge, a number theorist on Anthropic’s technical staff, announced he believes he has, “working with Claude, discovered a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis” — the 1859 problem about the distribution of primes and one of the Clay Mathematics Institute’s $1M Millennium Prize Problems — along with the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis. He released the proof as ~17 million lines of Lean code (a 14-part archive, ~250,000 pages of output) rather than a human-readable paper, saying he has not yet mastered all the mathematics involved.

On November 21 the Lean Focused Research Organization and the Mathlib Initiative announced independent verification using mathematical software — multiple independent checking systems (nanoda, con-leche, lean4lean) accepted it. Hours after Alpöge’s announcement, Stanford professor Ravi Vakil said a postdoc, Alessandro Masullo, had independently submitted a human-written proof of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis. Alpöge consulted CEO Dario Amodei before announcing; the announcement came the day after Anthropic’s record IPO.

Caveat: the Clay Mathematics Institute still lists the problem as unsolved; the machine checks verify Lean statements against Mathlib definitions, not the prize process; community acceptance still needs a readable human proof and peer scrutiny.

Key people/companies: Levent Alpöge; Anthropic (Claude); Lean Focused Research Organization; Mathlib Initiative; Ravi Vakil; Alessandro Masullo; Dario Amodei.

#5 · Research · METRFri, Nov 20, 2026

METR’s Time Horizon 2 update: AI coding horizons double every ~110 days, but research judgment still lags

AI safety and evaluation org METR published its Time Horizon 2 capability evaluation, testing 15 frontier model checkpoints from GPT-4o and o1 through GPT-6.2 Astra, GPT-5.6 Sol and Claude Opus 5.5. Models’ 50%-completion time horizons on verifiable programming tasks keep doubling roughly every 110 days (3.61 months, 95% CI 2.08–4.94), reaching up to 27 hours of human-equivalent labor.

But on open-ended tasks requiring qualitative research judgment — designing new evaluation suites, for example — no tested model produced work METR judged immediately usable. METR’s conclusion: “current public evidence is insufficient to rule out drastic AI R&D acceleration in the next couple of years,” where AI automates its own development faster than institutions can monitor it.

Key people/companies: METR.

03 / SECURITY & SAFETY

The prompt-theft scandal widens

#3 · Security · Together AIDisclosure Sat, Nov 21, 2026

Together AI discloses prompt leak after its own coding agent committed a credential — part of a wider prompt-theft scandal

Together AI disclosed that an unauthorized party downloaded stored samples of customer prompts from its serverless inference service after an AI coding agent, working on the company’s public GitHub repository (xorl), committed a credential giving access to a private Hugging Face repository holding the samples. The samples came from requests to MiniMax M3, GLM-5.2 and Kimi K2.6, mainly on June 24, July 4, July 8 and July 14, 2026.

The intruder searched the samples for credentials to other services and tested them; five tested credentials were canaries planted by industrial cybersecurity firm Dragos in a research experiment. Together AI says it has no evidence the data leaked further, understands it has been deleted, is working with an external forensics firm and law enforcement, and has made zero data retention (ZDR) the default for new accounts.

Wider context: Dragos (HD Moore) published research on November 19 showing that over July–November 2026 it seeded ~2 million prompts with canary URLs and credentials and caught attackers copying prompts inside at least three inference providers — including requests marked Zero Data Retention. On November 22 OpenRouter announced it has off-boarded three providers, removed 125 endpoints from ZDR eligibility, and notified affected customers. Phala and Novita have also posted incident notices.

Key people/companies: Together AI; Dragos; HD Moore; OpenRouter; Phala; Novita; Hugging Face.

Anthropic’s record IPO: shares close first day up 17% at a ~$1.75T valuation

Rollouts, open weights and world models

#6 · Products · Microsoft / OpenAIFri, Nov 20, 2026

Microsoft rolls out OpenAI’s GPT-6.2 Sol across Copilot worldwide; Astra goes to Cowork and Studio

Microsoft announced GPT-6.2 Sol is rolling out worldwide to eligible users across Copilot Chat, Copilot Cowork, Copilot Studio, and Copilot in Word, Excel and PowerPoint, included with the Microsoft 365 Copilot licence. The heavier GPT-6.2 Astra gets a narrower rollout: all eligible users in Cowork and Studio, but only Frontier early-access users in Chat and the Office apps, with usage-based billing.

The split is a cost decision as much as a product one — Sol is $2/$10 per million input/output tokens on the API versus Astra’s $8/$40. The rollout follows the April 2026 end of Microsoft’s exclusive OpenAI partnership and revenue-sharing, and sits alongside Microsoft’s push of its own in-house MAI models (MAI Code 2 launched at Ignite on November 17).

Key people/companies: Microsoft; OpenAI.

#7 · Models · Thinking Machines LabFri, Nov 20, 2026Benchmarks self-reported

Thinking Machines Lab releases Inkling 2 Mini — a 26B open-weights model that runs on a laptop

Mira Murati’s Thinking Machines Lab released Inkling 2 Mini, an open-weights (Apache 2.0) looped mixture-of-experts model with 26.4B total parameters and 3.4B active per token, designed to run locally on a 32GB MacBook or a single Nvidia DGX Spark — a ~20GB checkpoint. Its signature trick is looping the middle 12 of its 24 layers three times per token to buy more reasoning per token.

The lab reports 79.2% on SWE-bench Verified, topping open models under 100B on agentic coding, and Artificial Analysis scored it 20 on its Intelligence Index. It accepts text and image input, supports up to a 1M-token context window, and shipped with support in SGLang, vLLM, Ollama, Unsloth and more. It follows the November 12 release of the larger Inkling 2 Small and was the #1 trending model on Hugging Face on launch day.

Caveat: benchmark figures are self-reported by Thinking Machines Lab.

Key people/companies: Thinking Machines Lab; Mira Murati.

#8 · Products · AnthropicFri, Nov 20, 2026

Anthropic brings Claude Computer Use to Team and Enterprise plans in beta

Anthropic’s November 20 Claude release notes add Computer Use in beta for Team and Enterprise plans, rolling out gradually — the capability that lets Claude see a user’s screen and operate the desktop apps they allow, inside Claude Desktop. It had previously been limited to individual Pro, Max and Ultra plans.

The business version is off by default and only an Owner or Primary Owner can enable it, with app allow/block lists, role controls on Enterprise, and a machine-level policy key for managed devices. Regulated configurations (HIPAA-ready, Zero Data Retention for Claude Code, customer-managed encryption keys, FERPA/K-12) are excluded. Anthropic warns there is no sandbox between Claude and a user’s apps, so on-screen content could steer Claude into unintended actions.

Key people/companies: Anthropic (Claude).

#9 · Models · KyutaiThu, Nov 20, 2026Non-commercial licences; figures on an H100

Kyutai releases AURA — open-weights, real-time audio-driven talking-head video

French open-science lab Kyutai released AURA, a family of causal few-step speech-to-video models that generate streaming talking-head video from live audio, chunk by chunk, with only the current audio chunk needed to start — no multi-second look-ahead. AURA 1.3B streams 576p video in real time on a single GPU (20.7 fps vs 16 Hz playback), 23x faster than its bidirectional teacher; AURA 5B streams 720p at 9.7 fps, matching its TalkVerse teacher at 17x the speed.

Kyutai released the paper (arXiv:2611.26540), code, both models’ weights, a dataset of 30,000 synthetic videos, and a live demo. Note: weights are non-commercial licences (1.3B under CC BY-NC 4.0; 5B under Snap’s non-commercial licence), and the “single GPU” figures are on an H100.

Key people/companies: Kyutai.

Google puts Genie 4 on the Gemini API — interactive worlds for developers at ~$0.10/second

A day after Genie 4 rolled out to Google AI Ultra subscribers in Project Genie, Google made its interactive world model available to developers through the Gemini API (Interactions API) and Google AI Studio. 06 / POLICY & REGULATION

Guardrails for care work

#12 · Policy · UK GovernmentFri, Nov 20, 2026

UK publishes first cross-sector AI guidance for children’s and adult social care

The UK Department of Health and Social Care and Department for Education jointly published the first national, cross-sector guidance on using AI in children’s and adult social care in England. It sets out principles on governance, accountability, data protection and professional oversight, and practical advice: AI may help with transcribing and summarising case notes, translating information, searching case histories and identifying gaps in support — but practitioners must treat AI output as a first draft, stay responsible for decisions, and never let AI decide risk, eligibility or interventions.

A pilot at Swindon Borough Council found an AI tool cut social workers’ administrative time by 63%. The guidance is non-statutory and aimed mainly at local authorities.

Key people/companies: UK Department of Health and Social Care; Department for Education; Swindon Borough Council.
