01 / MODELS & RESEARCH

Price pressure meets the verification gap

#1 · OpenAI · GPT-6September 22, 2026Vendor-measured

OpenAI launches GPT-6 Sol and Luna, halving API prices—90 minutes after Anthropic

OpenAI expanded GPT-6 with Sol for everyday work and deeper reasoning at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, plus Luna for fast, high-volume work at $0.10/$0.50. Both are priced at half their GPT-5.6 predecessors.

OpenAI says Sol makes about half as many mistakes as GPT-5.6 Sol and matches or beats Claude Fable 5.1 on some benchmarks. Caveat: the accuracy and benchmark claims are based on OpenAI’s internal testing and have not been independently replicated here.

Why it matters

The 90-minute gap between Anthropic’s and OpenAI’s launches turned efficiency gains into an overt price contest for agentic workloads.

#6 · Qodo · Software engineeringSeptember 23, 2026Vendor survey

Qodo report: reviewing AI-generated code is now the top delivery bottleneck

04 / SECURITY & SAFETY

AI becomes both accomplice and attack surface

#1 · Microsoft DCU · PhishingSeptember 22, 2026

Microsoft disrupts EvilTokens, an AI phishing service that hit 12,000+ inboxes

Microsoft’s Digital Crimes Unit, Health-ISAC and partners including Cloudflare, Coinbase, OpenAI, SpyCloud, Shadowserver and TRM Labs seized 50 websites and disabled more than 150 domains behind EvilTokens. The service charged $1,500 upfront plus $500 a month.

Its chatbot analyzed stolen inboxes, selected targets, impersonated trusted contacts and drafted fraud messages. Device-code authentication sent victims to Microsoft’s real sign-in page while attackers stole sessions without seeing passwords. U.K. Metropolitan Police arrested two suspected administrators.

Why it matters

The case shows how AI can industrialize the judgment-heavy parts of phishing while familiar authentication flows preserve a false sense of safety.

#2 · Cisco Talos · MalwareSeptember 22, 2026No confirmed deployment

CLOSEDQUORUM lets four AI models vote on a malware implant’s next move

Cisco Talos analyzed a Windows implant that asks DeepSeek, Qwen, Mistral and Google Gemini to vote on tactics such as credential theft, injection, persistence and lateral movement; DeepSeek breaks ties. Talos calls it the first publicly documented implant with fully autonomous AI command-and-control and no human operator required.

Caveat: there is no confirmed in-the-wild deployment, and the analyzed binary shipped with dummy API keys. Talos also open-sourced CAIRN, the Cognitive Artifact Intelligence Research Network toolkit for hunting AI-integrated malware.

#3 · Meta Muse · macOSDisclosed September 21 · hot-fix confirmed September 22Single source

Meta hot-fixes a macOS Muse zero-day after Patrick Wardle’s public disclosure

Security researcher Patrick Wardle found that any unprivileged local process could rewrite an undocumented preference key in the new Muse Mac app, redirecting voice dictation and authentication tokens to an attacker-controlled server. Meta Superintelligence Labs’ David Singleton confirmed an emergency hotfix.

The flaw could turn a broadly permissioned agent into a local backdoor, but it required existing local code execution and was not a remote exploit. Caveat: the disclosure and hot-fix are covered here by one outlet.

05 / POLICY & REGULATION

Safety overtakes competitiveness in public concern

#1 · JUST Capital · Public opinionSeptember 23, 2026Organization-published survey

JUST Capital: AI safety now outranks competitiveness as the top public concern

In its fourth quarterly survey, 53% of corporate leaders, 49% of the public and 58% of investors ranked AI safety and security above U.S. competitiveness concerns, a pattern the organization says has held across four quarters.

Sixty-seven percent of Americans said the U.S. and China should cooperate on AI safety; 52% called AI a bigger threat than China, versus 32% taking the opposite view. The public holds AI developers and deployers equally responsible for safety, while corporate investment is lagging expectations. Caveat: the figures come from JUST Capital’s survey release.
