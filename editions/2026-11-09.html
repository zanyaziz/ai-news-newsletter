The lead story

Top Story · AppleNov 8Single-sourced — Gurman / Bloomberg, unnamed sources; Apple has not confirmed

Apple races to make Siri agentic

Apple CEO John Ternus has pressed software chief Craig Federighi to speed up Siri's roadmap and turn it into an assistant that works like an agent — software that follows a goal and keeps working on it without the user starting each step — according to Bloomberg's Mark Gurman in his Power On newsletter on Sunday, November 8. The goal is to rival OpenAI's Dots, Meta's Muse and Grok. Two upgrades are expected in the coming months: finer control over Apple's own apps in iOS 27.3, due in December (editing photos, organising Safari tabs), and third-party app integration in spring 2027, which The Verge says would let Siri place a Grubhub order or book an OpenTable reservation. Gurman believes a fully agentic Siri could arrive as early as 2027.

Apple has not confirmed the report or any timeline, and some coverage has urged caution — AppleInsider said the account should be taken with a grain of salt. Siri AI shipped in beta with iOS 27 on September 14, and Apple removed the waitlist on November 5. It matters because Siri is the assistant on more than a billion devices: if Apple turns it into a real agent, agentic AI goes mainstream overnight — but Apple is visibly playing catch-up to rivals that already ship agents.

Key players: Apple, John Ternus, Craig Federighi, Mark Gurman (Bloomberg).

02 / RESEARCH

Machine-checked proofs, and alignment for a new kind of model

#4 · AI for Science · OpenAI / Moonshot AINov 5 (arXiv), reported Nov 8Unreviewed research

GPT-6 Astra proves 70% of research theorems in Lean

A benchmark paper posted to arXiv on November 5, TCSExam, reports OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra producing complete, machine-checked Lean proofs for 70.0% of 20 research theorems in theoretical computer science — though it resolved none of the 30 open conjectures in the benchmark. Moonshot's Kimi K3.1 Pro came second at 38.3%, and the other six agents tested reached at most 11.7%. On the paper's combined TCSExam Index, GPT-6 Astra scores 71.9 and Kimi K3.1 Pro 57.4. It matters as a concrete, machine-checked measure of AI capability on real research mathematics — and as a gap: proving known theorems is not the same as cracking open ones.

Key players: OpenAI, Moonshot AI.

#5 · AI for Science · Physical Superintelligence / AnthropicNov 6–7, reported Nov 8Unreviewed · Model unidentified

A claimed proof of the Aaronson–Ambainis conjecture — with an unidentified Anthropic model

Physical Superintelligence (PSI) says instances of a non-public Anthropic model produced a complete proof of the Aaronson–Ambainis conjecture, accepted by the Lean proof assistant, during the third day of a run. The paper has not been published in a peer-reviewed journal, and Business Insider reported on November 7 that it is not clear which unreleased Anthropic model was used, with no public assessment yet from Scott Aaronson or Andris Ambainis. It matters if it holds up — a machine-checked proof of a named open conjecture would be a landmark for AI in mathematics — but the claim currently rests on the claimant's own account.

Key players: Physical Superintelligence, Anthropic.

#6 · Alignment · UIUCNov 8New technique · Preprint

PBPO brings RLHF-style alignment to diffusion language models

UIUC researchers introduced PBPO (Prefix-Block Policy Optimization), a new technique enabling RLHF-style training for non-autoregressive diffusion language models — a major step toward bringing alignment methods to an entirely different class of generative AI. Diffusion LLMs generate text in parallel rather than token-by-token, and existing alignment recipes built for autoregressive models did not transfer cleanly. It matters because diffusion models are gaining ground as a faster alternative architecture, and they need the same safety-tuning toolkit as today's mainstream models.

Key players: UIUC researchers.

06 / POLICY & SAFETY

Two lawsuits, one leak, and the training-data question

#16 · Copyright · SunoFiled Nov 6, reported Nov 8Allegations in filed complaints — not court findings

Elvis and Eminem rights holders sue Suno over AI training and voice imitation

Elvis Presley's rights holders and Eight Mile Style, the publisher of Eminem's catalog, have filed separate lawsuits against AI music company Suno in the Northern District of California. The Presley plaintiffs — including Authentic Brands Group and Raleigh Music Publishing — filed a 51-page complaint alleging Suno copied 859 compositions for training and lets users generate music imitating Presley's voice, invoking Tennessee's ELVIS Act, the 2024 law against unauthorized AI use of a performer's voice and likeness. Eight Mile Style's suit covers 269 Eminem compositions and seeks about $41 million in statutory damages; Eminem is not personally a plaintiff. Suno says it prohibits unauthorized use of artists' work and has measures meant to prevent it. These are allegations, not court findings.

It matters because it is the first major case to combine a training-data copyright claim with a voice-identity claim under a state likeness law — a two-front test of what AI music companies can legally build on and produce. Suno already faces litigation from Universal, Sony and Warner.

Key players: Suno, Elvis Presley Enterprises, Authentic Brands Group, Eight Mile Style, Eminem.
