01 / BUSINESS & MARKETS

The IPO meets its first sell-off

#1 · Company / Markets · AnthropicFri, Nov 20, 2026

Anthropic shares slide ~9% the day after its record IPO as tech stocks sell off

Anthropic (Nasdaq: ANT) fell 8.5% on its second day of trading, closing at $88.91 — still 7% above its $83 IPO price but down 20% from its $111.50 opening print, when it briefly topped a $2 trillion valuation. The slide came amid a broad tech rout: the Nasdaq fell 5.3% on Friday and gave up all its 2026 gains.

Underwriters exercised their overallotment in full, lifting total IPO proceeds to about $93.5 billion — the largest IPO on record — plus a $26 billion private placement to Google, Microsoft and Nvidia, for a total haul near $120 billion. Why it matters: the whiplash is the clearest stress test yet of AI valuations, and the next overhang is lock-up releases, with up to 315 million shares freed on day one and up to 1.7 billion more after annual results.

Key people/companies: Anthropic (Nasdaq: ANT); Google; Microsoft; Nvidia; Nasdaq.

02 / MODEL RELEASES

The densest open-weight week of the year

#2 · Models · Z.ai (Zhipu)Mon, Nov 23, 2026 · Late-breakingVendor claims only — no benchmarks yet

Z.ai launches GLM-5.4, its first Loop Transformer flagship, with vision — subscription-first, no benchmarks yet

Z.ai (Zhipu) released GLM-5.4, its first flagship built on a Loop Transformer: it runs 28 layers of its roughly 750B-parameter GLM-5 base twice for deeper reasoning without adding parameters, adds vision input for the first time in the mainline flagship, and offers a 1M-token context. It is live now for GLM Coding Plan subscribers, with 50% more weekly quota than GLM-5.3. No benchmark scores, API pricing, parameter table or open-weight date have been published.

Why it matters: the first flagship-scale deployment of the loop-transformer idea, with Z.ai claiming intelligence comparable to 2T–3T-parameter models from a ~750B base; the subscription-first, benchmark-free launch is a notable shift in how Chinese labs ship. Caveat: benchmark and capability claims are vendor claims only — no independent scores exist yet.

Key people/companies: Z.ai (Zhipu).

#3 · Models · Sarvam AISun, Nov 22, 2026Benchmarks vendor-reported

India’s Sarvam releases Sarvam-105B-1711, an open-weight 105B model that runs on a single GPU

Bengaluru-based Sarvam AI released Sarvam-105B-1711, an open-weight checkpoint of its 105B-parameter mixture-of-experts model (10.3B active per token), retrained with reinforcement learning on upgraded B200 infrastructure. Weights are on Hugging Face under Apache 2.0 in BF16 (198 GB) and a 65 GB NVFP4 build that Sarvam says fits on a single 96 GB RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell GPU.

Sarvam says average scores across eight conversational-agent benchmarks rose from 42.6 to 73.9, and blended API pricing fell from $0.44 to $0.15 per million tokens. Why it matters: the strongest open-weight enterprise-agent release of the weekend and a milestone for India’s domestic AI effort — a capable, cheap, single-GPU-deployable model aimed at high-volume customer support and voice agents, under a permissive license. Caveat: all benchmark scores are Sarvam’s own, not independently reproduced.

Key people/companies: Sarvam AI; Hugging Face.

#4 · Models · TencentFri, Nov 20, 2026Benchmarks vendor-reported

Tencent ships full Hy4 open weights — a 770B flagship, under a license with a $50M catch

Tencent published the full weights of Hy4, its flagship Hunyuan mixture-of-experts model: 770B total parameters, 49B active per token, natively multimodal (text, image, video), 1M-token context, with instruct, FP8 and base checkpoints plus an 82-page system card, on Hugging Face, ModelScope and GitCode.

The catch is the license: the custom Tencent Hy License v1.0, whose Section 10 requires a separate written license for model-as-a-service operators above $50M annual revenue or 100M monthly users. Why it matters: the largest open-weight model ever released, arriving the same week as Inkling 2 Mini and Sarvam-105B-1711 — the densest open-weight week of the year — but the custom license and 8-GPU hardware requirement mean it is open in name for most builders. Caveat: all benchmark claims are Tencent’s own, not independently reproduced.

Key people/companies: Tencent (Hunyuan); Hugging Face; ModelScope; GitCode.

03 / RESEARCH BREAKTHROUGHS

Small models, proven kernels

#6 · Research · MITMon, Nov 23, 2026

MIT’s SLIC trains a tiny model to design analog chips as well as frontier LLMs — locally

MIT researchers unveiled SLIC (Small LM Iterating on Circuits), a training method that teaches a small, few-billion-parameter language model to design analog integrated circuits as well as or better than frontier LLMs. Using multi-turn reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards against a circuit simulator, a 4B-parameter SLIC model reached 87.1% in-distribution Pass@1 on four-specification amplifier tasks within eight turns — 48.5 points above the 763B DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash Think — and beat GPT-6-Astra at the four-turn horizon (74.9% vs 67.1%).

Because the model is small and locally deployable, chipmakers can train on confidential foundry data without sending it to hosted services. The paper will be presented at NeurIPS 2026. Why it matters: analog chip design is one of the last bastions of expert human engineering, and the result is a striking counterexample to the scale-equals-capability narrative.

Key people/companies: MIT; NeurIPS 2026.

#7 · Research · CertiK / ColumbiaFri, Nov 20, 2026Bug severity unconfirmed

TensorLean brings mathematical proof to AI GPU kernels — and finds 25 bugs in DeepSeek and FlashAttention code

Researchers from CertiK and Columbia University introduced TensorLean, a formal-verification framework that mathematically proves production GPU kernels compute what they should, using machine-checkable proofs written in Lean over compiled NVIDIA PTX code. Applied to more than 100 production kernels, it found 25 bugs — two in DeepSeek kernels and 23 in FlashAttention implementations.

Why it matters: a silent numerical bug in a kernel can waste millions of dollars of compute or subtly corrupt a model, and TensorLean is the first serious attempt to bring aerospace-grade verification to the lowest layer of the AI stack. Caveat: the announcement did not include a full bug list or reproducible proof package, so the severity of the 25 bugs is unconfirmed.

Key people/companies: CertiK; Columbia University; DeepSeek; NVIDIA.

$5 billion, no product

Ex-Qwen chief’s Pragmatik Labs hits a $5 billion valuation just three months after its first round

Pragmatik Labs (p7k), the Shanghai AI startup founded by former Alibaba Qwen lead Lin Junyang, closed a new funding round valuing it at $5 billion — 2.5x the $2 billion valuation from its first financing in August. The new round raised only tens of millions of dollars, with new investors HyT Capital, CDH Investments and automaker Geely joining prior backers Gaorong Ventures, HSG, Tencent and the Shanghai Future Industry Fund. 05 / POLICY & REGULATION

Disclosure, not a ban

#9 · Policy · US GovernmentWed, Nov 18, 2026 · Circulated Nov 21–22Single-sourced

US weighs forcing companies to publicly disclose their use of Chinese AI models

The Trump administration is considering requiring US companies to publicly disclose their use of Chinese AI models — posting the information on their own websites — according to Reuters, citing three people familiar with the matter. Treasury and the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy have discussed the idea, which would apply to companies such as Airbnb (whose AI customer-service agent reportedly runs on Alibaba’s Qwen).

Why it matters: the first concrete policy proposal targeting the already-widespread enterprise use of Chinese open-weight models inside US products; disclosure rather than a ban keeps the administration’s deregulatory posture intact while putting model-supply-chain choices in public view. Caveat: single-sourced at origin (Reuters, three unnamed people); neither Treasury nor OSTP commented, and no formal proposal exists yet.

Key people/companies: US Treasury; White House Office of Science and Technology Policy; Airbnb; Alibaba (Qwen).

06 / COMPANY & PARTNERSHIPS

An MoU with no money attached

#10 · Company · OpenAITue, Nov 17, 2026 · In week’s roundups Nov 21–22

OpenAI for Chile: AI in public services, civil-servant training and startups — no spending commitment

OpenAI and Chile’s Ministry of Science, Technology, Knowledge and Innovation signed a memorandum of understanding — “OpenAI for Chile” — to explore AI adoption in public administration, AI literacy and upskilling for civil servants, and support for local AI startups. Signed by CEO Sam Altman and Minister Ximena Lincolao.

The MoU sets no investment or purchasing commitment; a joint task force will agree priorities and a workplan, and the initial phase has no cost to the Chilean state. Why it matters: the latest template for OpenAI’s government-by-government expansion — and a notably modest one: an MoU with no money attached, framed as exploration rather than deployment.

Key people/companies: OpenAI; Sam Altman; Chile Ministry of Science, Technology, Knowledge and Innovation; Ximena Lincolao.
