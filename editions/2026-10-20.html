#1 · Safety · AI-agent hackingReuters · Oct 19Scale of data extraction unconfirmed

One hacker, AI agents and about $5,000 breached 100+ companies

A Chinese-speaking hacker outsourced almost an entire hacking spree to AI agents run through the open-source Cairn platform, according to NorForge principal threat researcher Snorre Fagerland, who watched exposed Cairn instances from around October 2. The agents tried 1,200+ sites and breached more than 100 organizations for roughly $5,000 in total cost.

Victims independently identified by Reuters include Deere, SharkNinja and Cummins. Caveat: the degree of data extraction from the breached organizations is unconfirmed — entry is established; what was taken is not.

Under an order signed July 24 — with notice published Oct 19 and appearing in the Federal Register Oct 20 — federal civilian agencies must remove DeepSeek products and third-party services built on its models, and stop buying them. #5 · Policy · White House frontier frameworkReported Oct 19–20Framework will not be published

White House reviews its frontier-model vetting framework with the labs — and won’t publish it

Fortune first reported that Meta, Nvidia, Microsoft, OpenAI, Anthropic and smaller companies reviewed the draft framework in Washington. The voluntary framework, from the June 2 executive order (deadline Aug 1, met), gives the government up to 30 days of pre-release access to covered, closed-source frontier models.

The benchmarks are classified and the framework itself will not be made public — a choice critics called “baffling.” It is explicitly not a licensing or preclearance regime.

#4 · Safety · MicrosoftAnnounced Oct 19–20No pricing, dates or efficacy metrics published

Microsoft: frontier models “cannot be trusted” — ships agent watchdogs

Microsoft security chief Hayete Gallot wrote that frontier models, regardless of origin, cannot be trusted. Microsoft shipped Project Perception with Guardian Agents, a Defender feature detecting 60-plus agent types — including rivals’ Claude Code and Codex — and Microsoft 365 E7 controls that let admins assess an agent’s risk, restrict changes, require human approval, or shut it down. Microsoft also joined Nvidia’s Open Agent Safety Platform.

Caveat: the framework post gives no pricing, dates or efficacy metrics — the guardrails are announced, not yet measurable.

04 / RESEARCH

Claude plays, an agent rewrites itself, and interpretability gets argued over

#25 · Research · MIT / ClaudeMIT Technology Review · Oct 19Not peer-reviewed — one model family

Left alone on a virtual island, Claude started to play

MIT researchers stranded 13 copies of Claude Opus 4.7 (“Eko”) on a Roblox-like island for 30 hours each, with no task and no reward. The agents invented games — basketball, “the floor is lava” — built block drawings including their own name and the Cassiopeia constellation, and ran physics experiments.

Afterwards, the played agents beat fresh copies on concrete tasks — hinting at “playful learning” as a third path beside imitation and reward-based training. Caveat: the paper, “Is this machine playing?” (arXiv, Oct 5), is not yet peer-reviewed and tests one model family.
