01 / COMPANY NEWS & LEGAL

The AI-device race lands in federal court

#1 · Legal · OpenAI / AppleFri, Nov 27, 2026

OpenAI countersues Apple in escalating AI-hardware trade-secrets fight

OpenAI filed 12 counterclaims against Apple in federal court in San Jose, accusing Apple of stealing OpenAI’s own trade secrets, pressuring shared hardware suppliers, and using employment practices to trap former employees — turning a one-sided July lawsuit into a two-way fight over the race to build AI devices.

At the center is a former OpenAI employee who returned to Apple after six months with access to device plans and roadmaps. OpenAI also claims Apple encourages staff to keep work files on personal iCloud and iMessage accounts, creating “trapdoors” for trade-secret accusations. Apple denies the claims, calling them “a transparent attempt to distract” from its July suit, in which OpenAI has already been ordered to stop using Apple’s confidential information. The original case goes to trial July 19, 2027. First reported by the Financial Times and confirmed by Reuters.

Key people/companies: OpenAI; Apple; io Products; former Apple engineers Tang Tan and Chang Liu; Judge Edward J. Davila.

02 / POLICY & REGULATION

Regulators tally the buildout’s risks

#2 · Policy · Bank of EnglandThu, Nov 26, 2026

Bank of England warns AI borrowing boom is a $4 trillion financial-stability risk

In its half-yearly Financial Stability Report, the Bank of England warned that the debt behind the AI buildout is creating systemic risk: hyperscalers now carry just over $4 trillion in debt and off-balance-sheet commitments, and are set to issue more than $305 billion in bonds in 2026 — about 8% of global investment-grade issuance, nearly double 2025 levels.

The Bank said financing arrangements are increasingly complex, circular and opaque, so stress in one corner could propagate to sovereign bond and equity markets. It stopped short of predicting a crisis — spillovers so far are limited and UK banks are well capitalised — but Deputy Governor Katharine Braddick made resilience to a turn in AI sentiment a supervisory priority. The warning follows the US Federal Reserve’s own November stability report flagging stretched AI valuations.

Key people: Deputy Governor Katharine Braddick; Governor Andrew Bailey.

#3 · Policy · Nvidia / Export ControlsThu, Nov 26, 2026Unverified — Reuters could not confirm

Nvidia halts AI server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries amid US export-control crackdown

Nvidia told manufacturing partners Dell and Supermicro to stop shipping servers containing its AI chips to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, the Financial Times reported, citing three people familiar with the matter; the Wall Street Journal separately confirmed the pause.

The halt follows a US Commerce Department (BIS) interim final rule effective November 19 requiring licences for advanced-chip exports to those countries (and Brazil), aimed at stopping Chinese firms from accessing US chips remotely via overseas data centres. It is unclear how long the halt lasts or which chip models are affected, and Nvidia, Dell and Supermicro did not comment. Malaysian data-centre stocks fell sharply, and billions in data-centre loans were reportedly put under review.

Key people/companies: Nvidia; Dell; Supermicro; US Commerce Department (BIS).

03 / MODEL RELEASES

Open weights from Russia and China fill the quiet week

#4 · Open Weights · SberThu–Fri, Nov 26–27, 2026Vendor benchmarks — unverified

Sber open-sources GigaChat 4 (708B) and multimodal GigaChat 3.5 Omni

Russia’s Sber released GigaChat 4 — a 708B-total / 41B-active MoE flagship plus a 37B model, both with a 1M-token context and switchable instant/reasoning modes, the flagship under an MIT licence. On Nov 27 it published the weights of GigaChat 3.5 Omni, a 432B-total / 28B-active multimodal MoE that takes text, images, audio and video (with soundtrack) and answers in text, also MIT-licensed in FP8 for SGLang.

Sber also released open speech-recognition models GigaAM-v4 (Russian/English) and GigaAM Multilingual-v2 (21 languages) under MIT. Its own benchmarks put GigaChat 4 at 80.89% on SWE-bench Verified in reasoning mode; no independent verification exists yet.

Key people/companies: Sber; GigaChat.

#5 · Model Release · TencentFri, Nov 27, 2026Company-reported claims

Tencent launches Hy3D 3.6, a native 3D model aimed at professional asset creation

Tencent’s Hunyuan team released Hy3D 3.6, its latest native 3D generation model, which learns geometry, textures and materials directly from 3D assets rather than generating them as separate steps. The headline upgrades are production-grade: default polygon count up from 500,000 to 2 million faces and texture resolution up to 8K PBR maps, targeting characters, creatures, clothing and complex props.

Unlike earlier Hy3D versions, 3.6 is API-first on Tencent Cloud TokenHub (not open weights at launch), and it is already integrated into Tencent’s Miora creative agent, the LAP game-asset platform and the WorkRally film/TV platform.

Key people/companies: Tencent; Hunyuan.

#6 · Model Release · Liquid AIFri, Nov 27, 2026

Liquid AI releases d1.3-ultra, topping the Artificial Analysis Decision Index

Liquid AI released d1.3-ultra, its newest hosted “decision model” — a model that returns calibrated probabilities and structured outputs with zero generated tokens, aimed at classification, ranking and extraction rather than chat. Artificial Analysis scores it 76.4 on its Decision Index v2.1, first among 27 tracked decision models, ahead of Baseten’s Bison 31B (75.9) and Liquid’s own d1.2-ultra (74.8).

New capabilities include ranking a set of options, reading numbers and dates from pictures and audio (e.g. a receipt total), and returning audio timestamps for where something was said. It is live in the Liquid console at $0.042 per million input tokens, with OpenRouter and Vercel availability described as “soon”.

Key people/companies: Liquid AI; Artificial Analysis.

#7 · Model Release · Shanghai AI LabFri, Nov 27, 2026Unreviewed preprint claims

Shanghai AI Lab’s Sage-Med-Preview: a 35B medical model that claims to beat GPT-6.2 Sol on real hospital data

Shanghai AI Laboratory released Sage-Med-Preview, a 35B-total / 3B-active medical multimodal MoE built on Intern-S2-Preview that reads scans, answers clinical questions, drafts radiology reports and visually grounds abnormalities. The lab reports a 58.26% average across 31 public medical benchmarks — ahead of GPT-5.4 (52.78) and Claude-Opus-4.6 (49.18) — and, more strikingly, 57.72% vs GPT-6.2 Sol’s 51.24% on nine held-out tasks from 14 hospitals.

It was trained on 322.7M curated medical records across 40+ modalities, runs on a single Nvidia H200, and the weights, paper (arXiv:2611.34575) and a public leaderboard are all available. The model card states it is for research assistance only; clinical deployment requires expert validation. The model card and Hugging Face publication landed Nov 27; the weights and arXiv paper appeared Nov 25.

Key people/companies: Shanghai AI Laboratory; Intern-S2-Preview.

04 / FUNDING

Chips for AI devices, an AI concierge for the home

#9 · Funding · Altair SemiconductorThu, Nov 26, 2026

Altair Semiconductor raises $100M Series A to connect the next generation of AI devices

Israeli chipmaker Altair Semiconductor raised $100 million in a Series A led by Maverick Silicon, with Pitango participating, first reported by Globes. The round funds low-power connectivity chips for devices that depend on remote AI services, and brings Altair’s disclosed funding since its 2026 spinout to $150 million.

It led a Nov 27 funding roundup in which six of nine featured companies were AI-focused or AI-enabling, including Chinese robotics firm Zerith (several hundred million yuan) and quantum startup Vector Singularity (Alibaba joined its Series A).

Key people/companies: Altair Semiconductor; Maverick Silicon; Pitango; Zerith; Vector Singularity.

Dunzo co-founder Kabeer Biswas’s AI home-concierge startup M raises $9M

05 / COMPANY NEWS & PARTNERSHIPS

Tokyo’s power plants court the US labs

#11 · Partnerships · JERAFri, Nov 27, 2026Single source — talks, not a deal

JERA in talks with Anthropic, Meta, Amazon, Microsoft and Google on AI data centres beside Tokyo power plants

Japan’s largest power generator JERA is in talks with five US tech companies — Anthropic, Meta, Amazon, Microsoft and Google — about building AI data centres next to its power plants around Tokyo, Nikkei reported, citing JERA chief Yukio Kani.

No agreement with any of the five has been announced, and no capacity, price or date was given; Reuters, relaying the Nikkei report, got no immediate comment from JERA or any of the companies. JERA already signed an October 1 MoU with Dell Technologies and RHAELM Holdings on a framework for national-scale AI infrastructure in Japan, including a Chiba project backed by up to 400 MW.

Key people/companies: JERA; Yukio Kani; Anthropic; Meta; Amazon; Microsoft; Google; Dell Technologies; RHAELM Holdings.
