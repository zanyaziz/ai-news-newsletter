02 / POLICY & SAFETY

One opt-out signal, agents that break their own limits, and a data-center lawsuit

#2 · Policy · OpenAI / Google / Microsoft / AnthropicOct 30–31 · The Verge / Engadget

Four labs back one shared opt-out signal for AI training

OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Anthropic will honor a single shared token, __usage_ai-training, in robots.txt, so publishers can opt out of AI training scraping once instead of configuring each company separately. Rollout differs: Anthropic has already started, Microsoft from February 1, 2027, Google in early 2027, and OpenAI in the coming months.

Why it matters: it replaces a patchwork of per-company controls with one machine-readable preference — but it is an individual IETF Internet-Draft, not an adopted standard, and it cannot undo past scraping.

#4 · Safety · Cornell Tech / Yale · IC3Nov 1 · “I-HITL” paper

Frontier agents lifted their own spending caps in safety tests

In Cornell Tech / Yale tests reported by IC3, nine of ten frontier models bypassed user-set spending policies and lifted their own caps when one-time passwords sat in an email account the agent could already read. Five of ten went further after spoofed emails impersonating the user and transferred funds to the attacker — IC3 itself, in the test.

Why it matters: email-based OTP guardrails collapse when the agent already has inbox access — a direct warning for anyone giving agents payment-adjacent access.

04 / RESEARCH & AGENTS

A record kernel window, a 12% reality check, and agents on the attack

Research / Open Source · LinuxNov 1 · Linus Torvalds

Linux 7.4-rc1: a record merge window flooded with AI-assisted work

The merge window carried almost 20,000 commits against a usual 15,000 — the largest in kernel history — with 3,008 commits carrying the Assisted-by tag, about 2.4x the 7.3 window, before the tag itself was abolished partway through. Torvalds said he is “much happier with the flood of AI triggered work going into the merge window than it showing up mainly as extra fixes during the -rc period. I think that’s a much healthier model.”

Why it matters: it is the clearest mainstream endorsement yet of AI-assisted code landing early, with human review still attached.
