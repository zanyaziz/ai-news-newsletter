Lawsuits, an IPO race, and a record chip quarter

#1 · OpenAI · Child safetyOctober 14Allegations — not findings

Eight states sue OpenAI over ChatGPT child-safety claims

Attorneys general in Arizona, Arkansas, Idaho, Iowa, Kansas, Montana, Nebraska and West Virginia each filed state-court consumer-protection suits alleging ChatGPT was marketed as safe for children under 13 while allegedly coaching minors toward harmful behaviour. They seek penalties, disgorgement and product changes.

OpenAI says it has built age-specific protections, parental controls and break reminders, and wants to work with states on industry-wide policy. These are allegations in filed complaints, not findings by any court.

Anthropic briefs investors ahead of a possible IPO — reported Q3 revenue of $16.8B

Anthropic reportedly held an investor meeting on Oct 14, disclosing Q3 revenue of $16.8 billion, up 45% sequentially, and reported profitability, as talk grows of a pre-Thanksgiving listing while OpenAI points to 2027. Prediction-market odds that Anthropic lists first swung about 12 points to roughly 17%.

Neither company had a public S-1 on SEC EDGAR as of Oct 14, and the revenue and valuation figures are reported talk, not filed terms.

#3 · Google · Flagship modelAnnounced Oct 13 · Covered Oct 14

Google makes Gemini 4 Argon broadly available

Google released its flagship model to developers and businesses worldwide for complex multi-step work in coding, finance, law, cybersecurity and science — after months of delays, a cancelled Gemini 3.5 Pro, and a DeepMind leadership shakeup.

API pricing starts at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, doubling after November 30. Early users include Deloitte, LexisNexis, Ambience Healthcare and Isomorphic Labs; the Fairwind program gives vetted cybersecurity defenders a version without cyber guardrails, which raises its own governance questions.

#4 · OpenAI · Criminal complaintComplaint unsealed Oct 13Allegations — not a conviction

OpenAI engineer charged over a hidden recording device in an executive conference room

Federal prosecutors say an OpenAI engineer, Abdulrahman Alfozan, 35, was arrested on Oct 9 and charged with attempting to intercept oral communications after a concealed, encrypted audio recorder was found on Sep 30 in a TV USB port in a San Francisco executive conference room.

OpenAI says it fired him and has no indication model weights, source code or customer data were accessed. A detention hearing is set for Oct 19. These are allegations in a complaint, not a conviction.

#5 · ASML · EarningsOctober 14

ASML posts record quarter on AI chip demand and raises guidance

ASML reported Q3 revenue of €11.66 billion, up about 55% year on year, net income of €4.96 billion and a 57.5% gross margin, and raised full-year 2026 guidance to €44–45 billion with Q4 guided at €14.2–15.2 billion. Its CEO said customers are already discussing equipment orders for delivery in 2029 and 2030.

One caveat: profit was also lifted by valuation effects from ASML’s stake in Mistral AI, so the margin and order book are the cleaner signals.

#6 · xAI · Voice modelOctober 14Usage figures company-reported

xAI ships Grok Voice 3.0 for voice agents

xAI’s real-time speech-to-speech model is now in the Voice Agent API at $0.08 per minute, unchanged from the prior version; apps using the grok-voice-latest alias were migrated automatically and Grok Voice 2.0 is deprecated.

It tops the Artificial Analysis Speech-to-Speech Index at 86.9 — but on the tau-Voice simulated support-call benchmark it scores 71.6%, behind GPT-Live-1 at 74.5%, the test closest to the market it is sold into. xAI’s Tesla and Starlink usage figures are company-reported and unverified.

Representatives of Anthropic and OpenAI told a parliamentary committee that voluntary pledges are not enough for frontier-model safety, backing mandatory elements starting with incident reporting — while Google DeepMind and Meta did not support a new statutory framework. 05 / RESEARCH

How learning agents might carry skills between runs

#19 · EvoMap · Learning agentsPromoted October 14Beta report — unverified

Compact ‘strategy genes’ beat full skill documents for learning agents

A beta technical report (arXiv:2604.15097) reports that across 4,590 controlled trials in 45 scientific code-solving scenarios, a compact strategy-gene representation averaged 54.0% versus 49.9% for documentation-style skill packages, with distilled failure warnings helping most.

Flag prominently: this is a beta report promoted through syndicated press releases, not peer-reviewed or independently replicated.
