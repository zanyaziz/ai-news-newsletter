02 / AI SAFETY & PACING

The argument moves from warnings to brakes

AnthropicSept. 12Multi-sourced

Dario Amodei calls on AI companies to slow the pace of capability gains

In a 3,800-word essay, Anthropic’s CEO urged labs to “slow the pace at which we improve the capabilities of AI models” and proposed embedded third-party safety evaluators with full company access—a commitment Anthropic says it is adopting unilaterally. Sam Altman replied “we will do the same,” while Elon Musk posted “Dario is right,” turning a lab-level concern into an unusually public show of alignment.

Key people & companies: Dario Amodei and Anthropic; Sam Altman and OpenAI; Elon Musk and xAI; former Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon.

Why it matters

Frontier-lab leaders are moving beyond general safety pledges toward a concrete governance mechanism that would give outsiders continuing access to how advanced models are built and assessed.

Senate negotiators weigh a duty of care and power to block unsafe models

Negotiators are considering a legal duty requiring frontier developers to prevent “catastrophic risks,” with possible federal authority to stop releases deemed unsafe and a right for companies to challenge a block in court. The proposal could also preempt state AI laws on covered risks, making the early-stage talks a consequential test of who sets the national safety floor.

Key people & companies: U.S. Senate negotiators; Google, Anthropic and OpenAI as companies that could be covered.

03 / RESEARCH & BREAKTHROUGHS

A proof claim—and a model for the Moon

OpenAISept. 12 coverageSingle-sourcedContested · not independently verified

OpenAI claims an unreleased model solved the Navier-Stokes Millennium Prize problem

OpenAI published a 165-page, computer-verified proof that it says was produced by a model “significantly more capable” than GPT-6 Astra, using roughly 10,000 agents over about 88 hours and 130 billion output tokens. Fields medalist Timothy Gowers called it symbolically “a big moment,” but Anthropic-linked researchers published competing work and questioned whether OpenAI’s model trained on their unpublished sessions.

Key people & companies: OpenAI; Jakub Pachocki, Sebastien Bubeck, Noam Brown and Mark Chen; Anthropic researcher Levent Alpöge; NYU’s Tristan Buckmaster; Timothy Gowers.

Why it matters

If independently validated, the result would be the first AI solution to a Millennium Prize Problem and a landmark for multi-agent scientific research. For now, both the proof and the priority dispute remain unresolved.

IBM + NASASept. 10Single-sourced

IBM and NASA release an open-source Lunar Foundation Model

Trained on more than 30 data layers from nine instruments across four missions, the model identifies lunar ice deposits, craters and volcanic features up to 23% more accurately than widely used methods. Its output can inform Artemis landing-site selection and the search for water ice that could become rocket-fuel feedstock, showing how domain models can turn fragmented mission archives into operational science.

Key people & companies: IBM; NASA; the Artemis program.

05 / POLICY & REGULATION

Europe’s disclosure rules switch on

European UnionSept. 13Single-sourced

EU AI Act content-labeling rules take effect

Companies operating in or targeting the EU must now clearly label AI-generated or significantly altered images, audio, video and text using visible notices plus technical methods such as watermarks or embedded metadata. Meta, TikTok and Google have already introduced broadly aligned measures, while artistic, fictional and satirical content is generally exempt.

Key people & companies: European Union and European Commission; Meta, TikTok, Google, Nvidia, Apple and OpenAI.

Why it matters

The rules turn provenance from a voluntary platform feature into a market-access obligation, setting an implementation test for both model makers and content platforms.
