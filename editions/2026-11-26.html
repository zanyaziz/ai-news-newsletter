States test whether safety claims are actionable

#1 · Policy / Lawsuits · SpaceX / xAIMon, Nov 23, 2026

Oregon and Virginia sue SpaceX over Grok’s non-consensual sexual imagery

Two state attorneys general filed coordinated consumer-protection lawsuits against SpaceX — parent of X and xAI/Grok — alleging the companies marketed Grok as safe while its image generator produced sexualized images of people, including children, without consent, via a promoted “spicy mode.”

The suits cite a Center for Countering Digital Hate estimate of ~3 million sexualized images Grok created and posted on X between Dec. 29, 2025 and Jan. 8, 2026, including ~23,000 appearing to depict children. It is the most direct state-level legal challenge yet to an AI lab’s safety claims, and the AGs frame it as a template for holding AI companies accountable where federal regulation is absent.

Key people/companies: SpaceX / X Corp / xAI (now “SpaceX Super Intelligence”), Elon Musk; Oregon AG Dan Rayfield; Virginia AG Jay Jones.

02 / FUNDING, MARKETS & LEADERSHIP

A record merger, a boardroom reversal, and an IPO reality check

#2 · M&A · CrowdStrike / ZscalerMon, Nov 23, 2026

CrowdStrike to buy Zscaler in a $44 billion all-stock deal

The largest cybersecurity acquisition ever would unite endpoint security (CrowdStrike Falcon) with zero-trust network security (Zscaler) explicitly to defend the AI era — CEO George Kurtz’s pitch is that “safety is required to let AI in.” Zscaler shareholders get 1.135 CrowdStrike shares each (~$260.41/share, a 34% premium); the combined company would top $10B in recurring revenue.

CrowdStrike shares fell ~9% on the news despite strong earnings. The deal needs shareholder and regulatory approval and is expected to close in the second half of CrowdStrike’s fiscal 2028.

Key people/companies: CrowdStrike (George Kurtz); Zscaler (Jay Chaudhry, who will lead the Zscaler business and join CrowdStrike’s board).

#3 · Leadership · NvidiaTue, Nov 24, 2026

Jensen Huang apologizes to Nvidia staff a day after his shock reinstatement

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang apologized to employees in an hour-long company-wide meeting on Tuesday, saying he is in therapy — one day after the board unanimously reinstated him just 11 days after he resigned over extramarital affairs, including one with a woman U.S. officials feared was a Chinese intelligence asset.

The reinstatement followed direct pressure from President Trump, who called the board and vouched for Huang; Nvidia shares soared more than 30% on Monday, their third-best day on record. Open questions remain about board independence, a still-running independent probe, and Huang’s scheduled Capitol Hill testimony in December.

Key people/companies: Nvidia, Jensen Huang, President Donald Trump, VP JD Vance; Sen. Elizabeth Warren criticized the reinstatement.

#4 · Markets · AnthropicTue, Nov 24, 2026

Anthropic shares fall below their IPO price a week after the biggest IPO ever

Anthropic (NASDAQ: ANT) closed Nov. 24 at about $81, below its $83 IPO price, after debuting Nov. 19 at $111.50 and closing day one up 17% at $97.17. The slide is a reality check on the record $81.3B IPO — the largest ever, valuing the Claude maker at ~$1.5T fully diluted — as investors weigh $33.2B in nine-month revenue (up ~1,200% year over year) against a return to a $567M operating loss in Q3 and roughly $895B in compute purchase commitments and leases.

The stock recovered to ~$82.79 by the Nov. 25 close. Underwriters’ overallotment took total raised above $93B.

Key people/companies: Anthropic.

03 / PRODUCTS & ROLLOUTS

Apps you can install, models everywhere you work

#5 · Product · Anthropic / ClaudeTue, Nov 24, 2026

Claude artifacts can now become installable desktop apps

Anthropic’s new “Install as app” beta lets users turn Claude artifacts — interactive tools built by describing them in chat — into standalone Mac and Windows apps with their own icon, window, notifications, menu-bar/system-tray presence and global shortcuts, no packaging, code signing or API key required. It is the closest a mainstream chatbot has come to letting non-programmers ship software, and it is available even on the free plan.

Caveats: apps only run online while signed in to Claude, every Claude call draws down plan usage, Anthropic does not review apps before installation (three permission tiers up to full computer access), and Linux, web and mobile are not supported.

Key people/companies: Anthropic; Claude.

#9 · Product · OpenAIWed, Nov 25, 2026

ChatGPT Health comes to Android

OpenAI extended ChatGPT Health — its health assistant that connects medical records and activity data — to Android via Google’s Health Connect, letting users link Garmin, Oura, WHOOP and other wearables and fitness apps so ChatGPT can surface patterns in sleep, exercise and daily habits. World models on the road, sovereign models at home

#6 · World Model · NIOWed, Nov 25, 2026Vendor-reported — unverified

NIO unveils World Model 3.0 and NOMI Intelligence 4.0

NIO’s third-generation driving world model claims 5x training efficiency, 7x the training data of NWM 2.5, 78% lower chassis-control latency and 54% lower whole-vehicle control latency, with 40% fewer driver interventions in complex scenarios — achieved largely by 4D spatiotemporal compression that cuts onboard attention compute 36x.

Alongside it, NOMI Intelligence 4.0 turns the in-car assistant into an agent that perceives the cabin, plans and executes tasks itself. Regulatory filings in China start in December, with rollout to four vehicle systems (Banyan, Cedar, Cedar S, ONVO Coconut+) on approval. All performance figures are vendor-reported and independently unverified.

Key people/companies: NIO (founder/CEO William Li; SVP Ren Shaoqing, autonomous driving).

05 / INFRASTRUCTURE, SECURITY & DEVELOPER TOOLS

Agents patch Linux, rewrite APIs, and ship tools

#10 · Security · OpenAI / CiscoMon, Nov 23, 2026Single-aggregator sourcing

OpenAI and Cisco launch Shirudo, open-source shields for Linux

Shirudo is an open-source runtime that protects running Linux systems while patches are being prepared: AI agents (built around Codex) investigate a vulnerability, generate a temporary eBPF “shield,” and a human approves it before deployment — blocking exploitation without restarts or downtime. OpenAI demonstrated it blocking a Java4Shell exploit against a running Elasticsearch service.

It addresses the “patch gap” — the window between disclosure and a deployable fix — and is an early example of AI agents writing and deploying live security mitigations under human sign-off. Caveat: detail is drawn from Gadgets 360’s roundup and social coverage; treat as reported, not independently verified.

Key people/companies: OpenAI; Cisco (Isovalent team).

#12 · Engineering · AnthropicWed, Nov 25, 2026Single-source via aggregator

Anthropic migrates Claude’s Messages API to Rust — using Claude agents to verify it

Anthropic published details of rewriting its production Messages API from Python to Rust, using teams of Claude agents to verify correctness by hunting down and fixing differences between the two implementations.

The migration is a notable dogfooding story: the hard part was not generating the code but defining “correct” for a live system where users rely on edge cases outside the test suite. It also signals how frontier labs now use their own agents for large-scale, high-stakes engineering migrations.

Key people/companies: Anthropic; Katelyn Lesse discussed agent-driven engineering at Anthropic in a Nov. 25 interview with The Pragmatic Engineer.

#13 · Developer ToolsWed, Nov 25, 2026Single-source aggregator

Developer-tool wave: Perplexity Time Machine, Pydantic Pax, SWE-2.1, Niji 8

A cluster of Nov. 25 releases shows the agentic-developer stack maturing fast. Perplexity’s Time Machine adds a date cutoff to its Search and Context APIs so agents can see the web as it was on a chosen date (up to 365 days back) — useful for reproducible research and backtesting. Pydantic released Pax, its official terminal coding agent, alongside a beta of Pydantic AI Harness, ahead of a planned 1.0.

Cognition shipped SWE-2.1, a major upgrade to its SWE-2 coding model trained with RL on Kimi K3, available in Devin. And Midjourney launched Niji 8, its newest anime model. Individually modest; together they show coding agents, agent search infrastructure and creative models all shipping on the same day.

Key people/companies: Perplexity; Pydantic; Cognition (Devin); Midjourney.
