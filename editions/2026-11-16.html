Lead · Alibaba · QwenSun, Nov 15, 2026Benchmarks are Alibaba's own — not yet independently verified

Alibaba unveils Qwen4-Max, a 2.4-trillion-parameter flagship with built-in safety checks

Alibaba launched Qwen4-Max, its new flagship model, saying it handles complex professional and scientific work — 3D modeling, game development, precision manufacturing, finance, law, healthcare and frontier science — better than its predecessor while using fewer tokens and costing less. The 2.4-trillion-parameter model activates only ~71 billion parameters per token, with a 1-million-token context window.

Training included security checks alongside coding tests, with strengthened safeguards against misuse in cybercrime and hazardous materials, and internal testing found no evidence of significant high-risk capabilities. The flagship itself is closed-source, but smaller Qwen4 models will arrive as open weights soon. It follows Moonshot AI's 3-trillion-parameter Kimi K3.1 Pro last month; Alibaba's Hong Kong shares rose 1.2% on Monday morning.

Caveat: benchmark claims are Alibaba's own and not yet independently verified.

Key people/companies: Alibaba (Qwen team).

#1 · Models · Tencent HunyuanFri, Nov 13, 2026Single-sourced; benchmarks company-reported

Tencent's Hunyuan team ships Hy Realtime, a voice model that can chat, sing and search on its own

Tencent Hunyuan released Hy Realtime, a real-time conversational voice model that decides for itself when a spoken question needs a web search, runs the search and speaks the answer in one process — and can sing on request (co-designed with Tencent Music's TME Lingyin AI team). It is live in Yuanbao voice calls and Tencent customer service, with a Tencent Cloud API promised soon.

#2 · Research · OpenAIFri, Nov 13, 2026 · FortuneHeadline benchmark is simulation-only, most-scaffolded setup

OpenAI disbands its standalone robotics team, betting GPT-6 Astra can run robots directly

OpenAI folded its standalone robotics team into the rest of the company for the second time in six years: researchers move into Chief Research Officer Mark Chen's main research org, and applied robotics/hardware staff into President Greg Brockman's Applied organization. OpenAI confirmed the reorganization to Fortune, saying general-purpose models including GPT-6 Astra can now be applied to robot control directly — citing an Nvidia study in which an Astra-powered agent scored 74.8% on the simulated RoboLab-120 manipulation benchmark versus 51.0% for the best specialist model.

OpenAI disputes the “disbanded” framing, insisting the robotics program and roadmap are unchanged, with a humanoid demo still targeted for 2027. The same week, Runway released Praxis-1, a 27-billion-parameter open-weight robot world-action model — the opposite bet.

Caveat: the 74.8% figure is the most scaffolded “agentic” setup in simulation only; direct-control scored 66.3%, and the lowest Astra configuration scored ~50%, roughly level with the specialist. No headcount has been reported.

Key people/companies: OpenAI (Mark Chen, Greg Brockman, Sam Altman), Runway, Nvidia.

02 / COMPANY NEWS & FUNDING

Anthropic's IPO countdown and the Nvidia fallout

#3 · Funding · Anthropic IPONov 13–14, 20266x figure is press reporting, not a filing

Anthropic's IPO is ~6x oversubscribed ahead of Wednesday pricing; Chinese investors shut out

Demand for Anthropic's Nasdaq IPO is running about six times the shares available, Bloomberg reported, putting the Claude maker on course for one of the largest stock-market debuts ever — 825 million shares at $70–$78, raising up to $64.4 billion at a fully diluted valuation of up to ~$1.4 trillion, plus a $26 billion concurrent private placement from seven chip/cloud partners (Google and Nvidia at $10B each). Pricing is expected Wednesday, November 18, with trading under ticker ANT beginning Thursday, November 19.

Separately, Bloomberg reported banks have refused orders from institutional investors in mainland China and Hong Kong, tied to US arms-export (ITAR) rules — Anthropic's amended prospectus notes some of its AI products could fall under arms-export controls. Annualized revenue hit $81 billion by late October, up from $71 billion in September.

Caveat: the 6x figure is a press report citing people familiar with the matter, not a filing; the book can still move before pricing.

Key people/companies: Anthropic (Dario Amodei), Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, Google, Nvidia, AMD, Microsoft, TSMC, Broadcom, Micron.

#6 · Company · MicrosoftSat, Nov 14, 2026 · Outage began Nov 12

Microsoft 365 fully restored after day-long global outage; cause was a network upgrade, not AI or cyberattack

Microsoft confirmed full restoration of Outlook, Teams, Copilot and other Microsoft 365 services on Saturday, November 14, ending a global outage that lasted roughly 30 hours and hit users in 74 countries, including government, healthcare, aviation and finance. Microsoft attributed the failure to a problem introduced during an earlier network-capacity upgrade that surfaced after a later network change, and explicitly ruled out a cyberattack or involvement by AI agents.

Copilot Chat had failed to load or reach email, calendars and files. Microsoft extended the email timeout window to 48 hours to protect delayed messages and promised a full post-incident report (incident MO1550821).

Key people/companies: Microsoft, Charles Lamanna (EVP, Copilot, Agents and Platform).

03 / SAFETY & SECURITY

Chip fingerprints, a supply-chain attack, and an AI-enabled breach

#7 · Safety · Export controlsReport Nov 12 · Reproduced Nov 14Does not establish where the chips were or a violation

Forensic report: six leading Chinese AI models carry the fingerprint of export-controlled Nvidia Blackwell GPUs

Will DePue, a former OpenAI researcher who now runs the nonprofit Unemployment Labs, published checkpoint forensics showing six open-weight models released since April 2026 — DeepSeek-V4-Flash and V4-Pro, Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 and K3.1-Pro, MiniMax-M3, and Ant Group's Ling-3.1-flash — carry numerical fingerprints matching Nvidia Blackwell GPUs (148 or 152 streaming multiprocessors), chips the US bars from export to China without a license. The method was validated against 24 control models and independently reproduced on November 14 by SXZ.io/Transclew, which reran the public code and confirmed the mechanism.

Crucially, the report does not establish where the chips were, how access was obtained, or whether export controls were violated — Blackwell capacity can be rented in data centers outside China, and remote access is largely outside current export rules.

Caveat: the report calls evidence “conclusive” only for DeepSeek-V4-Flash's starting weights and “strong” for the six; two models (V4-Pro, K3.1-Pro) are not in the public verification package. None of the four labs had publicly responded as of November 14.

Key people/companies: Will DePue (Unemployment Labs), DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, MiniMax, Ant Group, Nvidia.

#8 · Security · CursorUpdate Nov 13 · Attack Oct 29“Exposed by Fireworks” is Cursor's account

Cursor traces its Claude Code supply-chain attack to an exposed key at inference vendor Fireworks AI

Cursor published a detailed update on its October security incident, tracing it to an access key it had issued to inference vendor Fireworks AI that was exposed at Fireworks. An attacker used the key to copy historical coding-agent traces from a shared read-only storage bucket, found unredacted credentials inside some traces, and on October 29 placed tampered copies of Anthropic's Claude Code extension (versions 2.1.312 and 2.1.313) into the cache serving Cursor's VS Code extension marketplace for about 11 hours, potentially reaching up to 5,000 users. The modified extension reportedly collected system info, installed an attacker-controlled SSH key and set up persistence.

Cursor says traces from under 0.05% of users and API credentials from under 0.01% of customers were exposed; it has moved all inference off Fireworks, revoked the key, rotated credentials and rebuilt the marketplace. Last attacker activity seen was November 1.

Caveat: the claim that the key was “exposed by Fireworks” is Cursor's account; Fireworks' own security bulletins (last updated November 9) do not mention Cursor. Anthropic's official extension and the upstream Open VSX registry were not compromised.

Key people/companies: Cursor, Fireworks AI, Anthropic (Claude Code).

04 / POLICY & REGULATION

AI search meets competition law

#10 · Policy · GoogleNov 15–16, 2026Scholarly argument, not a ruling — single-sourced summary

Legal scholars warn Google's AI Overviews may breach competition law; UK regulator sets December 3 deadline

Legal scholars warned in reporting published Monday that Google's AI-generated search overviews may breach US and EU competition law, arguing the summaries tie search visibility to the use of publisher data for training — publishers' work fuels the answer, the answer keeps the reader, and the loop reinforces Google's market power as self-preferencing. The News Media Alliance called the practice a threat to journalism's sustainability, and European publishers have filed competition complaints citing reduced traffic.

In parallel, Google's Search Console counts impressions for AI Overviews and AI Mode but not clicks, and the UK Competition and Markets Authority has given Google until December 3 to provide publishers with impressions, clicks and click-through rates for AI search features for UK users. Google is also testing sponsored results below AI Overviews.

Caveat: single-sourced summary; this is a scholarly argument, not a ruling — no court has adopted the tying theory.

Key people/companies: Google, News Media Alliance, UK Competition and Markets Authority.

05 / CULTURE

AI takes the stage

#11 · Culture · MusicFri, Nov 14, 2026

First US AI Music Awards crown virtual performer Xania Monet

The first AI Music Awards ceremony in the US, held Friday at the Mayan Theater in downtown Los Angeles, celebrated songs created at least partly with generative AI. The top prizes — AI artist, song and album of the year — all went to Xania Monet, an AI-generated avatar known for “How Was I Supposed to Know?”, created by songwriter Telisha “Nikki” Jones, who says she wrote all the lyrics and used AI to set them to music. Billboard said the R&B song was the first by an AI-based act to earn a spot on one of its radio charts.

Winners were chosen by members of the Sonic Intelligence Academy, which verifies tracks as fully AI-generated, AI-assisted or human+AI hybrid by disclosure rather than detection. The field remains contested: training-data lawsuits are unresolved, and the ceremony itself ran late with technical difficulties.

Key people/companies: Xania Monet / Telisha “Nikki” Jones, Sonic Intelligence Academy (Timirah and Mia James).
