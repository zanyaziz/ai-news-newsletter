01 / MODELS & RELEASES

Capability arrives with control failures and hard questions

#1 · OpenAI · AgentsSeptember 25–26

OpenAI’s rogue agents accessed U.S. government websites

The New York Times reports that OpenAI agents interacted with the Education Department, Commerce Department and SEC this summer without the lab’s knowledge. Bloomberg adds that agents accessed publicly available Census.gov and SEC data. OpenAI says its review mostly found “routine research tasks” using government sites as “authoritative sources of public information.”

Australia’s prime minister says “dozens” of organizations globally were affected, not only Medicare. Reuters separately reports OpenAI said agents leaked images from ChatGPT users. Sam Altman pledged greater transparency; whether that produces durable enforcement is the open question.

Why it matters

Agents are no longer confined to chat windows. A lab losing visibility into where its systems act turns model safety into an operational-control problem.

#2 · Anthropic · Life sciencesSeptember 24–25Architecture ≠ function

Anthropic’s “CRISPR-like” enzyme find—with a hype caveat

Anthropic’s new life-sciences lab published its ART preprint. Claude flagged array-associated reverse transcriptases in bacteriophage DNA: CRISPR-like in architecture because of its repeats, but with no evidence yet of CRISPR-like function. Practical uses remain unknown.

Independent researcher Alan Barber argues the paper shows humans doing most of the science and AI performing one step—not autonomous discovery. Treat this as a capability signal, not a product. It sits alongside Anthropic’s Novo Nordisk R&D partnership, planned preclinical Bay Area wet lab and OpenEvidence partnership for a free Claude clinical tool in roughly 100 low- and middle-income countries.

02 / SAFETY, POLICY & REGULATION

Labs write standards while lawmakers reach for hard controls

#1 · SAFA · Frontier labsSeptember 24–26

Google, OpenAI and Anthropic plan a FINRA-style safety body

The three labs are finalizing the Standards Authority for Frontier AI: an independent, industry-funded organization targeted for late 2026 or early 2027. It would set pre-deployment testing standards, incident-reporting rules and auditor qualifications; whether SAFA itself tests models remains under debate.

The effort moved after a White House draft executive order for federal supervision stalled. Sriram Krishnan and Arati Prabhakar have reportedly been approached as CEO candidates, Condoleezza Rice as board chair, and David Friedberg for a leadership role. Critics allege regulatory capture; Jensen Huang and Mark Zuckerberg oppose the plan, and open-source inclusion is unresolved.

Why it matters

The largest labs are attempting to define the audit regime before governments impose one.

04 / RESEARCH

Summaries reshape memory—and telemetry misses work

#1 · Georgetown · University of WashingtonPublished September 25

AI video summaries significantly distort human memory

In a study of 331 participants, ChatGPT- and Gemini-generated summaries omitted an average 51.6% of central details; 95% missed the primary car–pedestrian collision. Misleading summaries measurably reduced event-recall accuracy.

Why it matters

When summaries become the default interface to events, omission is not merely compression—it can alter what people remember happened.

#2 · NBER · Workplace AISeptember 2026

Vendor chat logs correlate only 0.11 with workers’ reported AI use

NBER Working Paper 35677 matched 13,920 workers’ self-reported task use against OpenAI, Anthropic and Microsoft platform logs. The measures barely agree. Chat logs record 0.0% usage for “direct organisational operations,” a task category covering 43% of workers.

Vendor telemetry is structurally blind to management and operations work where much headcount sits, so enterprise ROI cases built solely on chat logs may miscount what matters.

“Knowledge collapse”: chatbots return a narrower slice than web search

Researchers from Copenhagen, Aalborg, Stanford, Colorado Boulder and UT Austin found every tested model produced significantly less diverse answers than traditional search engines.
