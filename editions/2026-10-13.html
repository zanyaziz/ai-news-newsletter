02 / POLICY & SAFETY

Washington debates a safety board while London regains testing access

#1 · U.S. Senate · AI safety legislationOctober 12Proposal — not law

Senators introduce a federal AI safety bill built around a new AI Safety Board

Senators Mark Warner, Brian Schatz, and Andy Kim introduced the Artificial Intelligence Risk Management and Security Act of 2026. The bill would create an AI Safety Board inside the Department of Commerce to evaluate frontier-model risks, set metrics, publish formats for technical disclosures, and define secure testing environments.

The proposal lands alongside related measures from Senators Cantwell and Hawley as AI safety regulation gains momentum in Congress ahead of the midterms — though mandatory regulation is expected to draw White House opposition.

#2 · Microsoft · Regulation proposalOctober 12Proposal · Secondary summary

Brad Smith says AI has fewer safety rules than milk or washing machines

In a BBC interview published October 12, Microsoft’s president argued that AI is governed by fewer safety rules than everyday products, and proposed five measures: global safety rules, mandatory incident reports, victim notification after rogue-agent breaches or misuse, independent pre-release evaluators, and an emergency brake.

Caveat: this is a proposal, not enacted law. The secondary summary notes no detailed public response yet from OpenAI, Google, or Anthropic.
