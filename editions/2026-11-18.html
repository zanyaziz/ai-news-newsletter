01 / MODEL RELEASES

#1 · Models · OpenAITue, Nov 17, 2026Capability & safety claims are OpenAI’s own

OpenAI launches GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol

OpenAI released GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol in ChatGPT Work, Codex and the API on November 17. Astra is the frontier model for the hardest reasoning, coding and professional work; Sol is the cheaper everyday option. OpenAI says both get better results on demanding tasks with fewer output tokens, and Astra’s standard API prices fell 20%: $8 per million input tokens ($0.40 cached) and $40 per million output tokens, while Sol holds at $2 / $0.10 / $10. Astra ranks second on the independent Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index. Notably, OpenAI says Astra is the first released model to hit the “High” threshold on its own AI self-improvement scale — a level the company itself classifies as a risk. The release was paired with an updated Astra for Law legal model.

Caveat: the index ranking is independent, but capability and safety claims are OpenAI’s own, on its internal scale.

Key people/companies: OpenAI.

#2 · Models · MicrosoftTue, Nov 17, 2026Benchmarks are Microsoft’s internal — not independently verified

Microsoft unveils seven MAI models at Ignite 2026

At Ignite 2026 (November 17–20, San Francisco), Microsoft AI released a family of seven models for specialized workloads: MAI Code 2 and Code 2 Flash for software development (Code 2 is generally available in GitHub Copilot and Foundry; Microsoft says it delivers 98% of GPT-6.1 Sol’s coding performance at about 40% of the price), MAI Cyber 2 Flash for cybersecurity, MAI Image 2.7 and Flash for image generation (Image 2.7 tops Artificial Analysis’s image-editing leaderboard, per Microsoft), MAI Realtime 1 — its first live speech-to-speech model, from $0.01 per audio minute — Dragon Copilot Transcribe for medical transcription, and Microsoft-Decision-1.1 for decision-making. Alongside came Microsoft IQ (an enterprise knowledge platform), expanded Agent 365 agent governance, and Copilot Workspaces and Autopilot.

Caveat: performance and price comparisons are Microsoft’s internal benchmarks — not independently verified.

Key people/companies: Microsoft AI; GitHub Copilot.

#3 · Funding · Isomorphic LabsTue, Nov 17, 2026

Isomorphic Labs raises $5B Series C to design drugs with AI

London-based Isomorphic Labs — the Alphabet-backed AI drug-discovery company spun out of Google DeepMind and led by Demis Hassabis — announced a $5 billion Series C co-led by Greenoaks and Sequoia Capital, with Andreessen Horowitz among new investors and continued backing from Alphabet. It follows a $2.1B Series B in May, bringing total funding to $7.1B. The money scales its IsoDDE drug-design engine, which proposes and refines molecules before lab work, and moves its own pipeline toward clinical trials; the company reports positive preclinical results in oncology and immunology and designs medicines with Lilly, Novartis and Johnson & Johnson.

Key people/companies: Isomorphic Labs; Demis Hassabis; Greenoaks; Sequoia Capital; Andreessen Horowitz (a16z); Alphabet.

03 / INFRASTRUCTURE & CHIPS

Record earnings, record commitments

#4 · Earnings · NvidiaTue, Nov 17, 2026

Nvidia posts record $115.8B revenue in first results since Huang’s exit

Nvidia reported Q3 FY2027 revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% year over year and above its own $108B guidance and the ~$109.1B Wall Street consensus. Data-center revenue was $107.6B (+110%); net income $65.3B (+105%); EPS $2.70 vs $2.47 expected. Q4 guidance is ~$130B. It was the first earnings report in Nvidia’s history without Jensen Huang — the board dismissed him on November 12 and installed CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO — though the results reflect his tenure. Supply commitments jumped to $390B (from $279B), largely to secure memory, and total future commitments reached $518B. Kress said the company will apply stricter standards to payment guarantees for neocloud operators — a shift analysts say could curb so-called circular financing. Vera Rubin generated nearly $20B in its first quarter of production shipments.

Key people/companies: Nvidia; Colette Kress (interim CEO); Jensen Huang.

04 / POLICY & REGULATION / SAFETY

Robotaxis grounded, targets in simulation

#5 · Policy · WaymoTue, Nov 17, 2026

California grounds Waymo’s freeway rides nationwide

California’s DMV issued operating restrictions on November 11, made public Tuesday, November 17, blocking Waymo’s driverless cars from freeways — and Waymo pulled freeway access in all 15 cities where it operates. The DMV cited three robotaxis entering an active construction closure on U.S. 101 near Burlingame and a separate incident involving CHP vehicles on Highway 280. The suspension is temporary while safety is reviewed, but it removes one of Waymo’s most visible differentiators — driverless highway driving — nationwide.

Key people/companies: Waymo (Alphabet); California DMV.

Safety · Vals / BloombergTue, Nov 17, 2026Simulation details are second-hand

Frontier models picked civilians as targets in weapons simulations

Evaluation firm Vals tested whether frontier models would help choose targets from drone and satellite imagery. Models from Anthropic, Google, xAI, DeepSeek and OpenAI identified civilians, ambulances and a school as military targets, Bloomberg reported. The finding lands as militaries and defense contractors increasingly integrate frontier models into targeting and decision workflows.

Caveat: reported by Bloomberg via Vals; simulation details are second-hand in available coverage.

Key people/companies: Vals; Anthropic, Google, xAI, DeepSeek, OpenAI.
