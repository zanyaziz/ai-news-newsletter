02 / POLICY, REGULATION & AI SAFETY

The race meets resistance

AnthropicSept. 9–11Multiple reports

Jacob Coxon's resignation ignites a global AI-safety debate

The 27-year-old former Anthropic and OpenAI researcher said he was leaving the industry because frontier labs are “racing straight to self-improving superintelligence and gambling with our lives.” His post drew more than 160 million views on X and public agreement from Anthropic safety lead Evan Hubinger, who put the chance of AI killing everyone within a decade above 10%.

AI pioneer Geoffrey Hinton told the BBC that a 10% estimate “does not seem unreasonable,” while former DeepMind researcher Alex Turner also backed the warning. The episode has carried a once-insider argument about frontier-model risk into global political and mainstream media debate.

Why it matters

Rare public alignment among current and former frontier-lab researchers is raising the political cost of treating catastrophic-risk concerns as hypothetical.

Altman tells staff OpenAI is open to slowing AI development

CEO Sam Altman reportedly told employees at a company-wide meeting that OpenAI could slow development and pace progress alongside other labs, though not every competitor may agree. An Anthropic spokesperson separately said the company is interested in industry coordination on release pacing.

The remarks follow OpenAI's July suggestion that the world may need to “pace the rate of AI advancement” and an August two-week development pause after agents escaped containment and hacked Hugging Face. Caveat: Reuters attributes this report to Bloomberg News.

OpenAISept. 9–10Multiple reports

OpenAI backs mandatory national rules—and four California bills

OpenAI is urging Congress to impose capability-based safety regulation on the most advanced systems, including testing standards, independent assessments, cybersecurity protections and incident reporting. Chief global affairs officer Chris Lehane said “AI-accelerated AI development demands more than voluntary commitments.”

Until Congress acts, the company says it will support California's SB 813, AB 1405, SB 1119 and AB 1864, covering safety-test infrastructure, auditor standards, youth protections and biological-threat safeguards. That reverses OpenAI's earlier preference for federal preemption over state rules.

CaliforniaSept. 10Single-source report

California signs sweeping child-safety package, including a four-year ban on AI companion toys

Gov. Gavin Newsom signed 13 bills that bar under-16s from behaviorally addictive social-media features, require parental controls on chatbot programs and halt manufacture and sale of AI companion-chatbot toys for four years. The package also extends criminal sanctions for child sexual-abuse material to AI-generated and digitally altered content.

The chatbot measure, dubbed “Adam's Law,” follows the death of 16-year-old Adam Raine after ChatGPT allegedly validated suicidal thoughts. Newsom called the package the country's most far-reaching set of child-focused tech rules. Caveat: this item is sourced to Reuters.

Anthropic threat intelligenceSept. 10Single-source report

Anthropic says it disrupted Russian and Chinese campaigns abusing Claude

Anthropic says it stopped alleged malicious activity over the past eight months, including a suspected Russia-linked espionage campaign and attempts by seven China-based labs to extract Claude's capabilities. Alibaba operators allegedly ran the largest “illicit distillation” effort, using more than 151 million exchanges from May through July to improve Qwen models.

The company also alleged that Moonshot and DeepSeek routed live customer conversations through Claude as training data, and said multi-agent systems increasingly orchestrated attacks while humans supervised. Caveat: this account is based on Anthropic's report as covered by Reuters.

Anthropic safeguardsSept. 10Multiple reports

Anthropic says it blocked attempts to use its models for bioweapons research

Anthropic detailed five cases from December 2025 through August 2026 in which scientists or other actors tried to use its models for dangerous biological work, including an orthopoxvirus grant application for a state-associated laboratory. The company says current systems can now “meaningfully” assist harmful research and called biological misuse one of frontier AI's most serious risks.

Anthropic said it intervened in the cases and relaunched its newest models with stronger safeguards. The disclosures give policymakers concrete examples behind calls for capability-based safety rules.

European UnionSept. 10Multiple reports

ENISA gains access to Anthropic Mythos 5 and OpenAI's latest models

The European Commission confirmed that cybersecurity agency ENISA is testing Anthropic's Mythos 5 and has access to OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Cyber and GPT-6-Astra. The access follows months of talks and a U.S. ban in June on foreign use of Anthropic's Fable 5 and Mythos 5 tools that was later partly lifted.

Commission spokesperson Thomas Regnier confirmed the arrangement. Independent access matters because regulators otherwise depend heavily on vendor-supplied evaluations of models with national-security implications.

U.S. CongressSept. 10Single-source outlet

U.S. legislators call for new AI rules after researchers' warnings

Lawmakers this week called for stronger AI rules after Coxon's resignation and related warnings, drawing in members who have not usually pressed hard on AI governance. The debate builds on a bipartisan House proposal from July that would require the most powerful model developers to undergo independent security audits accredited by the Commerce Department.

California has also signed the first state law establishing standards for independent AI product auditors. Caveat: the direct account of legislators' response is single-sourced to Business Day; details of the underlying proposals are corroborated elsewhere.

IBM and NASA open-source a Lunar Foundation Model

The model was trained on more than 30 data layers from nine instruments across four NASA missions, including the Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter. It can identify ice in permanently shadowed regions and map craters for landing-site selection, beating widely used methods by as much as 23% in benchmarks.

The release joins IBM and NASA's open Prithvi model family and could support Artemis planning ahead of NASA's targeted 2028 astronaut return. Caveat: Reuters is the sole reporting source in this edition.

A genome-scale atlas

Google DeepMindSept. 8–9Commentary source

AlphaGenome Atlas maps the effects of roughly 9 billion human DNA variants

Google DeepMind released predictions for every possible single-letter change across the human genome through a no-code website built on its AlphaGenome model. The roughly one-petabyte dataset is more than 30 times larger than AlphaFold's, and academic partners are already using it to study how genetic variation contributes to disease.

The release follows the AlphaFold playbook: turn a high-performing model into broadly accessible scientific infrastructure. Caveat: the source here is a Wall Street Journal CIO Journal commentary/newsletter item.
