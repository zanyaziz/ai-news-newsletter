#1 · Top Story · NvidiaThu, Nov 12, 2026

Jensen Huang resigns as Nvidia CEO after 33 years

Nvidia's founder stepped down as president, CEO, and board member effective immediately, ending a 33-year run at the helm of the world's most valuable AI chipmaker. CFO Colette Kress, 59, becomes interim CEO while keeping her finance role, and longtime director Harvey C. Jones becomes the board's first-ever chair. The company's press release and SEC 8-K give no reason, but the resignation follows weeks of scrutiny over Huang's reported relationship with a Singapore-based tech executive whom U.S. intelligence officials suspect of spying for China, and a board investigation announced Nov 8 into whether Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence operation. Nvidia shares fell more than 13% on the news, and by Nov 13 the company's market value had dropped below $4 trillion for the first time since July 2025 — roughly $1.7 trillion of value lost in the longest weekly losing streak since 2012.

Key people/companies: Nvidia, Jensen Huang, Colette Kress, Harvey C. Jones.

#2 · Top Story · Anthropic IPOThu, Nov 12, 20266x figure is a press report, not a filing — book can still change

Anthropic's IPO is about six times oversubscribed ahead of a record listing

Bloomberg News reported that orders for Anthropic's IPO are running about six times the shares available, with bankers prioritizing existing backers and some buyers set for smaller allocations than expected. The offering is 825 million Class A shares at $70–$78, aiming to raise up to $64.4 billion at a fully diluted valuation of up to ~$1.4 trillion, with pricing set for November 18 and Nasdaq trading under ticker ANT starting November 19. The deal also includes a $26 billion concurrent private placement from seven chip and cloud giants (Google, Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft, TSMC). The filings show an $81 billion annualized revenue run rate as of Oct 31 — but also ~$900 billion in long-term compute commitments.

Caveat: the 6x figure is a press report based on people familiar with the matter, not a filing, and the book can still change before pricing. Also of note: Bloomberg reported the banks will not accept orders from Chinese and Hong Kong investors.

Key people/companies: Anthropic, Dario Amodei, Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, Google, Nvidia.

#3 · Top Story · OpenAIFri, Nov 13, 2026

OpenAI launches ChatGPT Wallet — agents can now pay for purchases

OpenAI introduced ChatGPT Wallet, a payment method that lets its "dots" personal agents complete purchases on a user's behalf. The agent never sees the real card details: the user adds a card once, reviews items/total/card before each purchase, and approves with a biometric check (Face ID or fingerprint), after which the card network issues a payment token. Built in partnership with American Express, Mastercard, and Visa, with fraud-prevention partners Forter, Riskified, Sardine, and Signifyd. It is rolling out gradually, initially only to U.S. ChatGPT Pro subscribers aged 18+ with eligible U.S.-issued cards. OpenAI says it eventually expects users to delegate purchases entirely within set spending limits and categories. This is the clearest step yet from AI as a shopping advisor to AI as a buyer — and a direct challenge to the card networks' and retailers' checkout control.

Key people/companies: OpenAI, American Express, Mastercard, Visa, Stripe, Adyen.

#4 · Top Story · PolicyFiled Thu, Nov 12, 2026 · San Francisco Superior CourtNo freeze ordered; no hearing date public; no case number yet

Four nonprofits sue five AI labs, asking a court to freeze frontier AI development

The Irreplaceable Education Fund, Public Citizen, the Heat Initiative, and The Alliance for Secure AI sued Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI, and SpaceX's AI division (SpaceXSI), asking a judge to bar them from developing or deploying any AI system more capable than their most capable systems as of Nov 12, 2026. The 63-page complaint makes a single claim under California's Unfair Competition Law, arguing the labs' work is a public nuisance, and leans heavily on the companies' own published warnings about catastrophic risk. The suit was filed by former New Jersey Attorney General Matthew Platkin.

Important caveat: no freeze has been ordered, no hearing date has been made public, the published complaint carries no case number yet, and none of the five companies responded to press requests for comment. The timing is pointed — Anthropic is due to price its IPO on Nov 18.

Key people/companies: Public Citizen, Irreplaceable Education Fund, Heat Initiative, Alliance for Secure AI, Matthew Platkin; defendants Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI, SpaceXSI.

#5 · Late-breaking · Export controlsPublished Thu, Nov 13, 2026Not a finding that any law was broken — see limits below

Forensic analysis finds banned Nvidia Blackwell chips' “fingerprint” in top Chinese AI models

Former OpenAI engineer Will DePue, now at consultancy Unemployment Labs, published a forensic analysis concluding that six Chinese frontier models — DeepSeek-V4-Flash, DeepSeek-V4-Pro, Kimi K3, Kimi K3.1-Pro, MiniMax-M3, and Ant Group's Ling-3.1-flash — were initialized on Nvidia Blackwell GPUs, which are barred from export to China without a U.S. license. The method matches deterministic weight-initialization patterns (Philox seeds via cuRAND) in the publicly released model weights against more than six trillion chip-layout/seed combinations; four models match Grace Blackwell GB200/GB300 systems. DePue estimates the training clusters cost over $500 million combined.

Crucial caveats: the fingerprint identifies the hardware that created the models' starting weights, not necessarily every chip used in training; it says nothing about where the chips were, who owned them, or how they were acquired — Blackwell systems can be rented from data centers outside China — and the report itself states “the results are not a finding that any law was broken.” The named companies had not commented at publication. The finding lands as Congress pushes to tighten chip-export rules, with at least four bills pending.

Key people/companies: Will DePue (Unemployment Labs), DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, MiniMax, Ant Group, Nvidia.

Agent infrastructure and automated research draw the big rounds

#6 · Funding · LangChainAnnounced Fri, Nov 13, 2026Usage figures are company-reported, not independently audited

LangChain raises $300M Series C at a $3B valuation

LangChain, the startup behind the LangSmith agent platform and the open-source LangGraph/LangChain frameworks, raised a $300 million Series C at a $3 billion valuation — more than double the $1.25 billion valuation on its $125 million round about a year earlier. The round was led by existing investor CapitalG (Alphabet's growth fund), with new investors GIC and DFJ Growth, plus Salesforce Ventures, Insight, QuantumLight, and MongoDB Ventures; total funding to date is $460 million. The money goes into turning LangSmith into the platform that runs, evaluates, and governs production AI agents; the company says 7,000+ organizations use LangSmith and it handles over 1 billion events a day (company-reported figures, not independently audited). New LangSmith capabilities will be shown at a Nov 20 event. The round is a strong signal that investor money is flowing from model-makers to agent infrastructure. WSJ first reported Nov 12.

Key people/companies: LangChain, CapitalG, GIC, DFJ Growth, Salesforce Ventures.

#7 · Funding · MirendilAnnounced Thu, Nov 12, 2026Training claims not independently verified; no valuation disclosed

Mirendil raises a $1 billion Series A to automate frontier AI research

Mirendil, which emerged from stealth in June 2026, announced a $1 billion Series A led by Kleiner Perkins and Clearlake Capital, with Google, Battery Ventures, M13, Pear VC, S32, and others participating — less than five months after a $200 million seed round. The company says one researcher working with agents pretrained its Mirendil-Base coding model from scratch in 3.5 months on under $10 million of compute, and sells Lor, a private-beta platform that runs hundreds of agents on research work for days at a time. Its post-trained model, Fern, is claimed to hold up to 10 million tokens of context.

Caveat: no valuation was disclosed, and the training claims (cost, timeline, context length) have not been independently verified or benchmarked — they are the company's own claims.

Key people/companies: Mirendil, Kleiner Perkins, Clearlake Capital, Google.

#8 · Products · DeepSeekFri, Nov 13, 2026

DeepSeek opens its app's full toolset via API

DeepSeek opened the server-side conversation tools powering its consumer app through the DeepSeek API: web search, deep-thinking search, file input, and web reading can now be switched on in existing APIs with a few parameters, with the service handling tool calls itself. Standalone APIs for search, web scraping, OCR, speech recognition (ASR), and text-to-speech (TTS) were also released, along with an MIT-licensed open-source demo chat frontend (github.com/deepseek-ai/chat-demo) that can import earlier DeepSeek conversations. The move undercuts OpenAI and Claude on web-search pricing and turns DeepSeek from a cheap model provider into a fuller application stack.

#9 · Models · Google / GitHubFri, Nov 13, 2026

Google's Gemini 4 Hydrogen lands in GitHub Copilot; six older models to be retired Dec 14

Gemini 4 Hydrogen — Google's fast, efficient new model, which scored 51 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, surpassing the larger Gemini 4 Argon, and is priced at $1.50/$7.50 per million input/output tokens — is now generally available in GitHub Copilot across the app, CLI, and VS Code for Pro, Pro+, Max, Business, and Enterprise users. #10 · Open Source · ggml-orgFri, Nov 13, 2026

llama.cpp 1.0.0 released — local AI's foundational runtime hits its first major version

Georgi Gerganov and the ggml-org maintainers (now at Hugging Face) released llama.cpp v1.0.0 (libllama) and ggml v1.0.0 (libggml), the portable C/C++ runtime that underpins much of local and on-device AI inference — 3.5 years after its initial commit. The 1.0 milestone adopts semantic versioning, so breaking, non-backward-compatible API changes will now be marked by major-version bumps. A headline feature: users running mixture-of-experts models larger than GPU memory can now set aside a cache for frequently used experts. For the local-AI ecosystem, this is a maturation marker for the tooling that lets models run off-cloud.

Key people/companies: ggml-org, Georgi Gerganov, Hugging Face.

#12 · Models · PerplexityFri, Nov 13, 2026

Perplexity open-sources its Decider decision models

Perplexity open-sourced Decider v1.3 and Decider Flash v1.3, models that decide which tool or source to use for a query. The headline capability in the new versions is “locate”: describe what you're looking for and the model returns the exactly matching passage. Perplexity recommends them mainly for reviewing large volumes of security-log data and for bug-fixing; they can be used free for a week on OpenRouter, where CEO Aravind Srinivas says they already handle a billion requests a month.
