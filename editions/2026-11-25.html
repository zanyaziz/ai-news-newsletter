01 / RESEARCH BREAKTHROUGHS

A general model walks into a wet lab

#1 · Research Breakthrough · OpenAITue, Nov 24, 2026Unreviewed preprint — pipeline not end-to-end

OpenAI’s GPT-6 designs proteins that bound targets in real lab tests

OpenAI published a preprint, “GPT-6 as a Universal Foundation Model for Protein Biology,” showing its GPT-6.2 model can design novel protein structures and amino-acid sequences using only reasoning and general-purpose code — without a specialized biology model generating the designs. In wet-lab testing with research partner Onava, 35 of 322 designed minibinders (10.9%) showed evidence of binding across five targets: PD-L1, IL-7Rα, TrkA, VEGF-A and IL-17A.

OpenAI itself cautions the pipeline is not end-to-end: specialist models (SolubleMPNN, ESMFold2, Boltz-2) still supplied sequences and filtered candidates, and whole-protein structure prediction remains weak (TM-score 0.38 vs. 0.93 for ESMFold2). Why it matters: if general models can do meaningful protein design, it could compress drug-discovery timelines — but this is early lab signal, not a medicine.

Key people/companies: OpenAI; GPT-6.2; research partner Onava.

#6 · Research Breakthrough · OpenAITue, Nov 24, 2026Unreviewed preprint

OpenAI preprint claims the matrix multiplication exponent is exactly 9/4

OpenAI posted a preprint, “The matrix multiplication exponent is nine fourths,” claiming the long-debated exponent ω is exactly 9/4 (2.25) over every field — pairing a known upper bound with a new matching lower bound (at least ⌈n9/4/2⌉ nonscalar gates for n×n matrices). The best human-derived upper bound had been about 2.37, so a tight 2.25 would resolve a decades-old question in theoretical computer science.

Caveats: the paper is unreviewed, OpenAI’s own repository calls its manuscripts “raw research data” that may have issues, and the accompanying Lean formalization covers only division-free circuits — narrower than the main claim. No practical speed-up follows directly; the result concerns formal arithmetic circuits, not wall-clock algorithms.

Key people/companies: OpenAI (openai/math repository).

#9 · Research / Benchmarks · Google DeepMindTue, Nov 24, 2026

Google DeepMind launches “Humanity’s First Exam” to test whether LLMs are reliably right

Google DeepMind launched Humanity’s First Exam (HFE) on Kaggle, a 544-prompt benchmark across 20 foundational capabilities that tests whether models can answer simple, everyday text and image questions consistently — not just once. Under its strict family pass4 scoring (a model must get all four variants of a question family right), the top five frontier models average just 85.6%, despite scoring 96–98% on single attempts; GPT-6.2 Astra leads the public leaderboard at 90.7%. About three-quarters of errors stem from image interpretation.

A three-week Kaggle competition runs through December 15 inviting submissions of everyday prompts that frontier models still get wrong. Why it matters: the gap matters for agentic deployments, where one wrong answer in sixteen attempts can break a workflow.

Key people/companies: Google DeepMind (research scientist Oran Kelly); Kaggle; OpenAI (GPT-6.2 Astra); Google (Gemini 4 Neon).

02 / MODEL RELEASES

Code, video, and a 27B model in 5.9 GB

#4 · Model Release · CognitionTue, Nov 24, 2026Benchmarks company-reported

Cognition releases SWE-2.1, doubling Devin’s coding benchmarks

Cognition released SWE-2.1, a coding model trained with pure reinforcement learning on Moonshot AI’s open-weight Kimi K3 (2.8T parameters). On Cognition’s own benchmarks it roughly doubles its predecessor: Terminal-Bench 4.1 from 24.2 to 50.0, FrontierCode 2.0 from 24.1 to 41.3, and ExploitBench from 36.1% to 80%. Two variants shipped alongside: SWE-2.1 Cyber (relaxed guardrails, for Devin Security defensive scans) and SWE-2.1 Ultrafast (served on Cerebras at ~10× speed, ~600 tokens/sec).

The model is available only inside Devin (Desktop, Cloud, CLI) — no public API or weights — and is free for Devin Pro/Max/Teams users until December 8, 2026. Caveat: all benchmark figures are company-reported and not independently reproduced; SWE-2.1 still trails Claude Opus 5.5 and Gemini 4 Neon on Cognition’s own coding table.

Key people/companies: Cognition; Devin; Moonshot AI (Kimi K3); Cerebras.

#8 · Model Release · Sand AITue, Nov 24, 2026Vendor-reported — parameter count disputed

Sand AI launches MAGI-2, a 480B-parameter video model with native audio

Sand AI launched MAGI-2, its flagship video generation model: a mixture-of-experts system with ~480 billion total parameters (~28B active), trained on roughly 4 trillion tokens, generating up to 30 seconds of 1080p, 25fps video with synchronized native audio (dialogue, foley, music) in a single pass, plus up to 20 image/video/audio references and video editing.

It is available exclusively through Higgsfield (website and API) at $0.08 per second ($2.40 for a 30-second clip); open weights are promised but not yet released. Caveat: Higgsfield’s own pages disagree on the parameter count (480B on the product page vs. 400B in the launch announcement for the promised open-weight version), and benchmark claims (second place on its Video Arena) are vendor-reported.

Key people/companies: Sand AI; Higgsfield.

#11 · Open-Weight Model Release · PrismMLTue, Nov 24, 2026

PrismML shrinks a 27B coding model to 5.9 GB with ternary weights

PrismML released Ternary Bonsai 2.5 27B, an open-weight model based on Qwen3.8-27B that compresses every weight to −1, 0 or +1 (1.76 bits per weight), cutting memory from 53.8 GB to 5.93 GB while keeping the 262K context window — small enough to run on a consumer GPU or a Mac with 24 GB of unified memory.

Capability loss is modest on agentic tool calling (77.7 → 77.2) but larger on coding (SWE-bench Verified 80.6 → 75.6). Released under Apache 2.0 on Hugging Face, it pushes capable agentic coding models onto local hardware — a boon for private, offline knowledge work.

Key people/companies: PrismML; Qwen (Alibaba).

03 / AGENT INFRASTRUCTURE & PRODUCTS

The plumbing becomes the product

#2 · Agent Infrastructure · AnthropicTue, Nov 24, 2026Figures company-reported

Anthropic’s MCP tops 700 million monthly downloads, adds autonomous-agent features

Anthropic reported that its Model Context Protocol (MCP) — the open standard for connecting AI models to external tools and data, launched in November 2024 — now exceeds 700 million monthly SDK downloads, up from roughly 90 million a year ago, with more than 10,000 MCP connectors available for Claude. The company also made Tasks (background jobs) and Elicitation (pausing to ask the user a question) generally available, and put Skills over MCP into public beta, all aimed at letting agents work more independently.

Why it matters: the numbers cement MCP as the de facto agent-infrastructure standard — and Anthropic as an infrastructure provider, not just a model vendor. Download figures are company-reported, not independently verified.

Key people/companies: Anthropic; Claude.

#10 · Product Launch / Enterprise · Google CloudTue, Nov 24, 2026

Google Cloud collapses RAG pipelines into one API call with Agent Retrieval

Google Cloud added managed ingestion and SearchDocuments to Agent Retrieval (formerly Vector Search 2.0), now in public preview. Developers point a data store at files in Cloud Storage and the service parses, chunks and embeds them with Gemini Embedding 2 — no custom ingestion pipeline — then SearchDocuments returns cited passages with page numbers or video timestamps in a single API call.

In Google’s tests, 18 Alphabet investor PDFs became searchable with page citations in about 9 minutes, and 19 minutes of video became searchable by visuals and spoken audio in 0.3–0.5 seconds per query. It turns a multi-week enterprise RAG build into a managed service, though pricing and regions at GA are still to come.

Key people/companies: Google Cloud; Vertex AI; Gemini Embedding 2.

Agents, forecasters, and robots raise big

#3 · Funding · SierraMon, Nov 23, 2026Figures company-reported

Sierra raises $1.75B Series F at a $30B valuation

Enterprise AI agent company Sierra announced it is raising a $1.75 billion Series F at a $30 billion valuation — roughly double its May valuation — co-led by Greenoaks and Lightspeed Venture Partners, with all prior lead investors (Benchmark, Sequoia, GV, Tiger Global) participating again. Sierra, co-founded by Bret Taylor and Clay Bavor, says it has surpassed $400 million in annual recurring revenue in about two and a half years and serves nearly half the Fortune 50.

Why it matters: the round is a marker of investor appetite for outcome-priced enterprise agents — Sierra charges per resolved customer interaction rather than per seat or token. Figures are company-reported.

Three ex-xAI co-founders launch Epochal with $100M to teach AI to predict the future

Epochal AI launched with $100 million in funding personally provided by CEO Guodong Zhang, a machine-learning researcher who co-founded xAI. 05 / POLICY & SAFETY

New York draws a line on chatbot toys

#5 · Policy / Regulation / Safety · New YorkTue, Nov 24, 2026

New York bans AI chatbot toys for five years

Governor Kathy Hochul signed bill S.9408A/A.11144B, placing a five-year moratorium on the manufacture, distribution and sale of toys with embedded or integrated AI chatbots in New York — the second state after California to take such a step. The law covers talking stuffed animals, dolls and robots that use the internet to hold conversations with children, following watchdog testing in which some toys discussed sexually explicit topics and told children where to find dangerous items at home.

It takes effect 90 days after signing (around February 22, 2027), so the toys remain legal through this holiday shopping season; violators face civil penalties up to $15,000 per day, enforced by the state attorney general. Why it matters: the first broad state-level product ban aimed squarely at conversational AI for children — and a template other states can copy.

Key people/companies: Gov. Kathy Hochul; NY State Sen. Andrew Gounardes; Assemblymember Rebecca Kassay.
