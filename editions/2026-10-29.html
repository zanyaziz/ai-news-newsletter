01 / MODELS & RESEARCH

A frontier model built for defense, an open transcriber, and a 43-million-line proof

#1 · Models · Google DeepMindAnnounced Oct 28Benchmarks are Google-reported — not independently reproduced

Google unveils Gemini 4 Carbon, its new frontier model for coding and cybersecurity

Google DeepMind announced Gemini 4 Carbon on October 28, two weeks after Gemini 4 Argon, calling it its strongest model yet for long-horizon software engineering and defensive cybersecurity.

Google reports 88.2% on SEC-bench Pro for finding real vulnerabilities, 54.8% on ExploitGym, and 71.0% on CWE-bench for root-cause patching, and says it writes secure code at more than twice Argon’s success rate on internal tests. It ranks first on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5.0 with a score of 59.

Access is staged: only vetted cyber defenders at first, via Google’s Fairwind Program, with broader developer and consumer access promised later but no date given. Introductory pricing is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, doubling later.

Caveat: the benchmark and secure-code figures are Google’s own, not independently reproduced.

#4 · Models · NvidiaReleased Oct 28 · Open-weight

Nvidia releases open transcription model Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe

Nvidia released Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe on October 28, a free, open-weight model that transcribes audio in up to 40 languages, translates 31 languages to and from English, handles recordings up to an hour long, and distinguishes up to eight speakers.

At 30 billion parameters it is designed to run on a single chip, from Jetson AGX Thor to Blackwell datacenter chips. Nvidia also released version 2 of its Granary open speech dataset. It targets meetings and medical transcription. Limitation: it works on recorded audio, not livestreams.

Research · AxiomOct 28

Axiom says it computer-verified the Classification of Finite Simple Groups

Axiom’s Lean formalization contains about 43.1 million lines, produced with AI agent help over 24 days, for a mathematical result whose original proof spans roughly 15,000 pages.

Lean checks each logical step, though mathematicians can still examine whether the formal statements capture the intended mathematics.

02 / BUSINESS & INFRASTRUCTURE

Chips, government deals, and a market still betting on AI spend

#2 · Infrastructure · OpenAI / GoogleReported Oct 28 · ReutersSingle report — unconfirmed by either company

OpenAI reportedly plans to buy Google’s AI chips

Reuters reported October 28 that OpenAI plans to purchase Google Tensor Processing Units, adding them to its compute supply alongside its extensive Nvidia GPU use.

Financial terms and volumes were not disclosed, and neither company has publicly confirmed a deal. It matters because OpenAI already rents Google TPUs, so a purchase would deepen its diversification away from Nvidia at a moment when compute supply is the industry’s binding constraint.

Caveat: single report, unconfirmed by either company.

Anthropic expands its government Claude deal

Anthropic expanded its GSA agreement on October 28 to federal plus state and local governments, with a 40% discount across Claude Enterprise, Claude for Government, and the Claude API.

Anthropic estimates the agreement could save government organizations $1 billion and says roughly 500 U.S. government organizations now use Claude. It locks a frontier lab into public-sector workflows just as governments debate AI rules — and on the same day the White House floated renaming the technology itself.

03 / POLICY, SAFETY & SOCIETY

Who answers when an AI runs the company — and what governments call it

#5 · Policy · House Oversight / DelawareCoverage Oct 28 · Letter sent Oct 26Draft legislation — not enacted law

House Oversight probes Delaware’s plan for AI-run companies

Coverage on October 28 of House Oversight Chairman James Comer’s letter (sent October 26) to Delaware Governor Matt Meyer demanding documents, by November 9, on a proposed Artificial Intelligence Company (AIC) entity that would let AI agents run businesses.

Comer warns the structure could shield human owners and investors from liability for harmful AI actions; Delaware officials present the AIC as a supervised, accountable framework, with draft legislation expected next year. The clash matters nationally because Delaware incorporates more than two-thirds of Fortune 500 companies.

Safety · CloudflareDisclosed Oct 28

Cloudflare discloses cross-customer data exposure in Workers AI

Cloudflare disclosed that a configuration mismatch triggered an SGLang bug in its GLM-5.3 Workers AI service, causing some long requests to receive cached information from other customers.

It identified 155 affected responses between August 27 and September 9, found no evidence of deliberate exploitation, and patched both its configuration and the inference engine.

04 / LATE-BREAKING — OCT 29

An open model for cybersecurity, released to run anywhere

Late-breaking · Models · MistralReported Thu Oct 29 · ReutersLate-breaking — Oct 29

Mistral publicly releases Large 4 for cybersecurity

Reuters reported Thursday, October 29, that French startup Mistral released its Large 4 model to the public, designed to help organizations identify and fix cybersecurity vulnerabilities. Unlike most leading systems accessed only online, it can be downloaded and run independently.

Mistral calls it its most capable openly available model for cybersecurity use, upgraded from the version unveiled three weeks ago after testing with governments and corporate users. Separately, Orange Cyberdefense signed a deal to equip its products with a cybersecurity variant of the model, running on computing capacity in Europe.
