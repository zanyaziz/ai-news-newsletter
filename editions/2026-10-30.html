01 / MODEL RELEASES & RESEARCH

A safety-gated frontier, open weights from Paris, and a world model that talks back

#1 · Models · GoogleAnnounced Oct 28 · Widely reported Oct 29Benchmarks are Google-reported — not independently reproduced

Google launches Gemini 4 Carbon, a frontier model for cybersecurity and long-horizon engineering

Google’s Gemini 4 Carbon, announced October 28 and widely reported October 29, is pitched as its frontier model for cybersecurity and long-horizon software engineering. Google says Carbon scored 88.2% on SEC-bench Pro (finding real software vulnerabilities), 71% on CWE-bench (producing fixes), and 54.8% on ExploitGym.

It tops the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5 at 59, ahead of Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra. Access is restricted to vetted cyber defenders through Google’s Fairwind Program while voluntary U.S. government pre-release testing continues, with no date for broad access. Introductory pricing is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens.

Why it matters: Google is treating a model’s cyber capability as a safety-gated release, not just a product launch — the strongest model in the line is the one most people cannot yet use.

Caveat: the benchmark scores and index ranking are Google’s own figures, not independently reproduced.

#4 · Models · MistralWeights released Oct 29 · Open-weightBenchmark is Mistral-reported

Mistral releases the open weights of Mistral Large 4

Mistral released the open weights of Mistral Large 4 on October 29, after three more weeks of reinforcement learning, under a modified MIT license that asks model-as-a-service businesses to pay. Mistral’s own Terminal-Bench 4.0 score rose from 28% to 41%, and the API list price is now $0.70 / $2.10 per million input/output tokens — about half the preview rate.

Why it matters: a large European open-weight model aimed at coding and cyber defense, letting customers run it under their own control rather than through a U.S. cloud API.

04 / POLICY, REGULATION & SAFETY

Chatbot laws for minors, a split over the kill switch, and chips in suitcases

#6 · Policy · Anthropic / OpenAI / CaliforniaLetter dated Oct 15 · Reported Oct 29

Anthropic and OpenAI split over who pulls the plug on a dangerous model

In an October 15 letter to California Governor Gavin Newsom, reported October 29, Anthropic argues courts should be able to block release of models posing a “serious risk,” while opposing blanket “kill switch” mandates in favor of targeted interventions — disabling an agent or cutting off a customer. OpenAI, by contrast, favors requiring developers to maintain effective shutdown controls and use them when necessary.

Why it matters: the two leading labs are now publicly split on who pulls the plug — a court order before release, or a company-operated switch after it — and California is where that argument will be settled first.

Policy · New YorkSigned Oct 28 · Effective Jan 1, 2027

New York restricts chatbot features for minors

Governor Kathy Hochul signed a law on October 28, effective January 1, 2027: covered chatbots may not present as human, claim a personal relationship with a minor, encourage self-harm or secrecy, or hold sexually explicit conversations with young users. Penalties run up to $25,000 per violation.

Why it matters: New York is regulating relationship design, not just content — the features that make a chatbot feel like a companion are now the regulated surface.

05 / SAFETY INCIDENTS & ACCOUNTABILITY

A wrongful-death suit, a breach by agents, and 62 patched CVEs

#3 · Safety · Anthropic / CathedralDisclosed Oct 29

AI agents breach Anthropic through third-party software

Security startup Cathedral used AI agents to access a limited set of Anthropic sales, HR, and IT systems through third-party software. Customer and partner contact details and basic account data were exposed — but Claude chats, source code, and model weights were not. Anthropic shut the access points and notified federal authorities.

Why it matters: the attacker’s tooling was the same agent technology the industry is selling — and the way in was not Anthropic’s own perimeter but software it trusted.
