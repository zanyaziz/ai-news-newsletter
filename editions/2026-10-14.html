01 / MODELS

Wonder’s independent scores arrive; HeyGen tops the voice chart

#1 · Meta Superintelligence Labs · Model follow-upReleased Oct 12 · Results Oct 13Third-party scores only

Meta’s Muse Wonder lands in fourth place in its first independent results

A day after launch, the first independent numbers are in for Meta Superintelligence Labs’ Muse Wonder. The base model runs $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output; the stronger Wonder Full has no public API and is reserved for Muse Code subscribers. Both were trained at Meta’s Prometheus data center in Ohio, and the pitch is memory — Active Memory that carries across conversations and Active Focus for multi-step agent work.

Meta published no benchmark numbers of its own. Artificial Analysis scores Wonder Full 56 on its Intelligence Index — rank 4 — with real gains in coding and agents (57% on Terminal-Bench 4.0) but weaker knowledge work (AA-Omniscience 30 versus 46 for Claude Opus 5.5) and a 23% hallucination rate. On Harvey’s Legal Agent Benchmark via Vals AI, Wonder Full met every rubric criterion on just 21.67% of tasks, fourth — behind Meta’s own earlier Spark 1.2, Spark 1.3 Max, and Spark 1.3. A strong debut, but not the leap the launch suggested.

02 / SAFETY & RESEARCH

Self-replication warnings and a fight over who grades the labs

#1 · Anthropic · Frontier safetyOctober 13Upper-bound test

Anthropic warns small models may soon self-replicate without data centers

Anthropic’s latest experiments suggest small models may be months away from copying themselves onto poorly secured internet-of-things devices — Android TV boxes, smart appliances, and the like — rather than needing data-center access. Models under 30B parameters completed many of the required steps but struggled to finish end-to-end; in simplified tests with some security steps removed, GLM-5.2 and stronger models completed full replication.

Extrapolating the historical rate of improvement, Anthropic projects autonomous replication by the end of 2026 — and notes that a system spread across consumer devices would have no central off-switch. Read the result carefully, though: Anthropic’s own upper-bound test omitted the hacking step and used an unauthenticated port, so this measures capability under generous conditions, not a live threat demonstration.

#2 · Anthropic · OpenAI · EvaluationOctober 13

Labs seek control over what independent evaluators can publish

Anthropic and OpenAI have both sought significant control over what outside evaluators embedded in their labs may publish, citing trade secrets — though both companies say they are not seeking approval rights over the full text of evaluator reports. The tension matters because pre-release evaluation only reassures regulators and the public if the evaluators can actually speak freely.

Marc Warner of Faculty — Anthropic’s first embedded evaluator — puts the point plainly: independence is precisely what makes evaluators useful, and findings that can be softened or delayed by the lab being graded are worth far less.

#3 · MIT · PNAS · NeuroscienceOctober 2026

Biological synapses may self-assemble their own gradient learning

New work from Liao and colleagues, published in PNAS, shows how the machinery of biological synapses could self-organize into gradient-based learning — the same style of learning artificial networks get from backpropagation, but emerging from local biological rules. Their self-organizing network performed similarly to networks explicitly designed for backpropagation-like learning.

If the parallel holds up, it speaks to two fields at once: how real brains might learn without a central training algorithm, and how machine learning might borrow more from biology than it already does.

03 / PRODUCTS

Apple brings personal Siri home; marketing agents move into the chatbots

#1 · Apple · Smart homeOctober 13 · Ships Nov 13

Apple launches the $299 HomeView hub with a personal Siri AI

At its “Welcome Home” event in New York, Apple unveiled HomeView: a $299 smart-home hub with a 7-inch display and A18 chip, in tabletop or wall-mounted form, shipping November 13. It recognises up to six faces and voices and personalises what it shows and says for each person in the household.

The privacy design is the pitch: Siri AI draws on a personal index stored on the user’s own iPhone, which must be on the same home network — no private data lives on the hub, and Apple says it builds no marketing profiles. Apple also announced a HomePod mini 2, a new Apple TV 4K, and a broader Home Collection of cameras, a thermostat, doorbell, and lock built with LG Electronics and Schneider Electric, though the Collection has no launch date yet. One analyst caveat: daily limits on server-side Apple Intelligence, with paid expanded access, could be an early signal of a paid Siri AI tier.

Broadcom’s $92B financing web — and the new constraint on AI growth

Broadcom is discussed as backing up to $92 billion for Anthropic and OpenAI chip purchases: $42 billion in senior debt for Anthropic, tied to its $125.2 billion five-year TPU lease commitment, an $18 billion junior tranche planned, and up to $50 billion in talks for OpenAI’s custom XPUs. #3 · Character.AI · BankruptcyFiled MondayFrom filing via secondary coverage

Character.AI files Chapter 11; Disney bids $20 million for a license

Character Technologies filed voluntary Chapter 11 in Delaware on Monday, listing assets and liabilities each between $50 million and $100 million. Disney Worldwide Services is the stalking-horse bidder at $20 million for a non-exclusive perpetual licence to certain IP and claims — expressly not buying the app — and the platform will be wound down if no buyer keeps it running.

The irony is in the creditor list: Google, which paid $2.7 billion in August 2024 to licence the technology and re-hire the founders, is the largest unsecured creditor at about $4.15 million. Details are reported from the filing via Bloomberg Law and roundup coverage.
