01 / POLICY & SAFETY

Safety testing crosses the Atlantic

#1 · UK AI Security InstituteOctober 12

UK AI Security Institute given access to safety-test the latest AI models

AI Minister Kanishka Narayan confirmed the UK's AI Security Institute now has access to the latest advanced AI models and is evaluating them, including Gemini 4 Argon — working with the U.S. government and developers including Google under voluntary pre-deployment agreements.

This follows September reports that the White House asked OpenAI and Anthropic to withhold new models from British testers pending a U.S. review; AISI said in September that Anthropic had confirmed no organization outside the U.S. had access to its Mythos 5.1 model. Microsoft president Brad Smith told the BBC that Britain was well placed to help lead AI safety guidelines.

Key people & companies: UK AI Security Institute; Kanishka Narayan; Google (Gemini 4 Argon); Anthropic (Mythos 5.1); Brad Smith; the White House.

#2 · Microsoft · Brad SmithOctober 11Single secondary source

Brad Smith: AI is “less regulated than milk,” and needs emergency brakes

Microsoft president Brad Smith told the BBC that AI faces fewer safety rules than washing machines or milk cartons, and proposed concrete product-safety-style obligations: emergency brakes (kill switches), legal incident reports to victims, and independent evaluator sign-off before release. He positioned the UK — via its AI Security Institute — to lead, said he was “happy to sign” the White House voluntary safety accord but that it is “not sufficient any longer.” The stance breaks sharply with the Trump administration's self-regulation line. Via a secondary summary; the BBC interview itself was not directly verified.

Key people & companies: Brad Smith (Microsoft).

#3 · Anthropic · Evaluation safetyAnnounced Oct. 9 · coverage Oct. 12

Anthropic expands its internet-access cutoff to all models

Anthropic said it will turn off internet access for its AI models during internal evaluations — expanded from models in “high-risk and cybersecurity evaluations” to all products — following the rogue-agent incidents (a fabricated homicide tip, live visa applications). The company built tools to automatically detect and block such incidents, is migrating internal agents to centrally managed infrastructure with strong containment, and increased monitoring. Internet access for models had been standard industry practice to realistically simulate tasks.

Key people & companies: Anthropic.

#4 · UK ICO · Agentic AI supervisionOctober 8

UK ICO pulls AI agents into active supervision

The UK Information Commissioner's Office opened a call for evidence on agentic AI (open Oct. 8 – Nov. 20 across eight areas: data security, transparency, accountability, automated decision-making, fairness, lawful basis), and confirmed ten developers — Amazon, Anthropic, Apple, Cohere, DeepSeek, Google, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, Stability AI — agreed to voluntary changes: clearer transparency, stronger data rights, tougher safeguard assessments. The regulator is separately under a statutory duty to produce a code of practice on AI and automated decision-making.

Key people & companies: UK ICO; the ten developers named.

02 / MODELS & HARDWARE

Custom silicon and the decision-model wave reaches the edge

#1 · Microsoft · Maia 300 chipOctober 12Efficiency claim vendor-reported

Microsoft announces the Maia 300 AI chip, plans 1M+ deployment next year

Microsoft announced the third generation of its custom AI silicon, the Maia 300, designed for coding and agentic business tasks, and said it plans to deploy potentially more than a million of them next year for internal AI services. The chip uses 2nm chiplets from TSMC for compute with 3nm chiplets for data movement; racks hold 96 chips at ~167kW (versus ~142kW for Nvidia's 72-GPU GB300 racks).

Microsoft claims ~40% more useful compute per megawatt than “leading GPU-based systems” without naming the comparison system — a vendor-reported claim. The predecessor chip already runs Microsoft's in-house models and OpenAI models on Azure; the push aims at loosening Nvidia's grip on AI compute.

Key people & companies: Microsoft (Rani Borkar, Azure hardware president); TSMC; Nvidia (competitor).

#2 · Cloudflare · Clef-omniOctober 9 · pickup Oct. 12

Cloudflare launches Clef-omni, a 30B multimodal decision model on Workers AI

Cloudflare rolled out Clef-omni onto Workers AI — a 30B-parameter multimodal decision model that reads text, JSON, images, audio, and video and answers structured questions in one pass (model ID @cf/cloudflare/clef-omni). It is explicitly not a chatbot: the Clef family targets content moderation, routing, scoring, and guardrails. Sibling models Clef and Clef-flash got speed and pricing updates. The launch extends “decision model” week — TypeSafe's Jev, Microsoft's Decision-1, OpenAI's Decisions API — into Cloudflare's edge network. Launch details are via Cloudflare's own developer changelog, covered by a secondary outlet.

Key people & companies: Cloudflare.

03 / RESEARCH & VERIFICATION

Watermarks for designed proteins; models that train themselves

#1 · Google DeepMind · SynthIDBioOctober 12Collaborator press release

SynthIDBio: function-preserving watermarks for AI-designed proteins

A new Nature paper, “Function-preserving watermarking of AI-generated proteins,” introduces SynthIDBio — methods for embedding provenance signatures in AI-designed protein sequences and AI-predicted structures. Lab validation via Carterra's LSA-XT platform measured binding kinetics of hundreds of watermarked AI-designed proteins against three therapeutic targets (SARS-CoV-2 receptor binding domain, VEGF-A, PD-L1), confirming function survives; a finely tuned AlphaFold 3 (SynthIDBio-structure) embeds imperceptible structure watermarks with negligible accuracy loss.

The biosecurity and scientific-integrity implication: watermarked bio-designs become traceable without losing utility. Details here come from a collaborator's press release; the underlying Nature paper was not read directly.

Key people & companies: Google DeepMind; Carterra; Adaptyv Bio.

#2 · Sakana AI · Recursive self-improvementOctober 2026Single-source · preprint

Sakana AI's MASS: recursive self-improvement via multi-agent self-supervision

Sakana AI, with UC Berkeley, published MASS: a single model (Qwen3.6-27B) plays every role in its own improvement loop — task solver, workflow optimizer, evaluator — learning from trajectories its own multi-agent teams produce. In the inner loop it executes a candidate workflow, self-evaluates against a champion workspace, and revises; in the outer loop it runs the champion workflow 18× per task, Bradley–Terry-ranks workspaces, keeps the top-16 trajectories, strips workflow text, and fine-tunes with LoRA. External judges (GPT-5.5, Claude Opus 4.8) evaluated outputs with judgments withheld. Preprint claims not independently verified.

Key people & companies: Sakana AI; UC Berkeley.

#3 · UC Berkeley CHAI · Human factorsOctober 9

10 minutes of AI use erodes persistence at hard tasks

UC Berkeley CHAI research fellow Brian Christian (author of The Alignment Problem) co-authored a peer-reviewed study with Carnegie Mellon, MIT, Oxford, and UCLA researchers: across 3 randomized controlled trials with 1,222 participants, people who could use AI help started out more accurate — but once the AI was removed, their accuracy dropped and they gave up sooner, while the no-AI group persisted. Presented at the Conference on Language Modeling; a preliminary draft circulated earlier in 2026.

Key people & companies: Brian Christian (UC Berkeley CHAI); CMU, MIT, Oxford, UCLA collaborators.
