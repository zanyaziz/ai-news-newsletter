01 / FUNDING & INFRASTRUCTURE

Compute and coding agents pull in fresh billions

#1 · Crusoe · Atreides · Mubadala · ValorSeptember 17, 2026

Crusoe raises $3.9B at a $30.9B valuation

AI infrastructure provider Crusoe closed a $3.9 billion Series F at a $30.9 billion post-money valuation, underscoring the continuing flood of capital into AI compute. The round was co-led by Atreides Management, Mubadala Capital and Valor Equity Partners, with backing from Founders Fund, Nvidia and QIA.

Crusoe reports more than $140 billion in total contracted value and over 6 GW of contracted capacity, with 1 GW operational. Related megadeals include Blackstone and Alphabet’s Crux AI securing a $22 billion chip loan plus $5 billion in equity.

Why it matters

This is the biggest recent neocloud raise and a vivid measure of how aggressively investors are financing AI’s physical infrastructure.

Key players: Crusoe; Atreides Management, Mubadala Capital, Valor Equity Partners, Founders Fund, Nvidia and QIA.

Factory raises $200M at a $5B valuation, tripling since April

AI coding-agent startup Factory raised $200 million at a $5 billion valuation — more than triple its $1.5 billion valuation from April — with backers including Blackstone, Khosla Ventures, Sequoia Capital, Insight Partners, Evantic Capital and Sound Ventures. Founded in 2023 by Matan Grinberg and Eno Reyes, Factory builds AI agents for enterprise engineering teams, competing with Cognition and Cursor.

The raise highlights AI-assisted coding as one of the most heavily funded generative-AI use cases; Cognition raised $2 billion at a $48 billion valuation earlier in September.

Key players: Factory founders Matan Grinberg and Eno Reyes; Blackstone, Khosla Ventures, Sequoia Capital; competitors Cognition and Cursor.

02 / AI SAFETY & GEOPOLITICS

The safety debate collides with government practice

#3 · National Archives · Alibaba Qwen · FBI · AnthropicSeptember 17, 2026

Federal Register used Alibaba’s Qwen AI search despite FBI accusation

The Federal Register website, run by the National Archives, offered an AI search tool powered by Chinese company Alibaba’s Qwen model, even though the FBI last week accused Alibaba of “malicious” copying of U.S. rival Anthropic’s technology. The Qwen-powered search was removed Wednesday after social-media posts flagged it.

Experts said the use likely posed no direct national-security risk but contradicted Washington’s own messaging on Chinese AI models, arriving just ahead of a U.S.–China leaders’ meeting.

Key players: U.S. National Archives and Federal Register; Alibaba’s Qwen; the FBI; Anthropic and Dario Amodei.

King Charles warns AI executives of “existential dangers”

King Charles told leaders from Nvidia, Google DeepMind, OpenAI, Anthropic and others that the “existential dangers” of AI falling into the wrong hands require safeguards before “it becomes too late.” He warned that the technology risks “developing darker capacities — perhaps even to take life.”

Amazon said labs should release models “only when they’re ready and safe to use,” requiring “rigorous testing and strong safeguards,” but stopped short of calling for a slowdown. 04 / RESEARCH BREAKTHROUGHS

Research papers become agents

#9 · Stanford · Nature · Paper2AgentSeptember 16, 2026 · Published in Nature

Paper2Agent turns papers into interactive AI agents

Stanford researchers published Paper2Agent in Nature, a framework that converts a research paper plus its code and datasets into an interactive AI agent via an MCP server. Scientists can query, reproduce and extend the work in plain language instead of installing dependencies.

Across 100 computational-biology papers it averaged about 91% accuracy on paper-derived questions. In one demonstration, two paper-agents collaborated to flag a previously unreported MPHOSPH9 variant tied to ADHD risk, pointing toward a future in which large numbers of paper-agents collaborate at scale.

Key players: Stanford researchers James Zou, Jiacheng Miao, Jonathan Pritchard and colleagues; Nature; the jmiao24/Paper2Agent GitHub repository.

Why it matters

The work shifts scientific papers from static records into software-like interfaces that can be queried and combined, potentially compressing the path from published result to reproducible follow-on research.
