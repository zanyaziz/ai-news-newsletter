01 / MODELS & RELEASES

Open weights get a compliance shape

#1 · Aleph Alpha · Open weightsOctober 3

Kolibri: a 78.1B open-weight English–German MoE with only 3.46B active parameters

Europe's sovereign-AI push gets a concrete artifact. Kolibri is a Mixture-of-Experts transformer built end-to-end in Germany, shipping under Apache 2.0 on Hugging Face with 78.1B total parameters but only ~3.46B active per token, up to ~1M-token context (262k recommended for production), and per-request reasoning-effort control. The FP8 checkpoint is ~78GB and runs on a single B200/B300/H200 or 2× H100, served via vLLM.

It is explicitly positioned for regulated sectors — public administration, industry, aerospace — with a data pipeline that redacts PII pre-training and targets EU AI Act / GDPR / GPAI Code of Practice compliance (Aleph Alpha is a signatory). That makes it less a leaderboard play than a compliance-shaped alternative to U.S. frontier models for EU deployments. Performance comparisons are the company's internal evaluations, not independently verified.

Key people & companies: Aleph Alpha; Hugging Face; the vLLM ecosystem.

02 / RESEARCH

Privacy you can audit; physics as a check on models

#1 · Google Research · Federated learningOctober 2

Google details externally verifiable federated learning for Gboard

Google researchers described a federated-learning method where phone training data is encrypted and processed inside a server Trusted Execution Environment (TEE), with permitted processing methods logged in public records for external verification, and differential privacy applied. It was applied to Gboard's next-word prediction models for English and Japanese.

The significance is the verifiability claim: instead of “trust us, it's private,” the system aims for externally auditable proofs that only approved computations ran. If the pattern holds up, it is a template for improving on-device models without centralizing raw user data. Privacy and performance figures are Google-reported.

Key people & companies: Google Research.

#2 · Tohoku University · Materials AISept. 29 – Oct. 2

Physics-grounded materials AI framework proposes testable predictions

PhysMat AI, published in Advanced Functional Materials, argues materials discovery cannot rely on data correlations alone and organizes physical knowledge into five roles — prior knowledge, descriptors, constraints, verifiers, and infrastructure — so AI predictions become more interpretable and testable against physical law. It is a framework and perspective rather than a benchmark breakthrough, but it addresses a real failure mode: models that interpolate well and extrapolate into physically impossible materials. For labs doing AI-driven materials screening, the verifier layer is the practical takeaway.

Key people & companies: Tohoku University WPI-AIMR (Hao Li); Advanced Functional Materials.

03 / AGENTS & PRODUCTS

Agents move from demos to deployment shapes

#1 · IBM · DigitalOcean · PhotonOctober 2–3

Agents go operational: IBM Bob self-hosted, DigitalOcean Managed Agents, Photon raises $4.5M

The agent story is shifting from demos to deployment shapes. IBM launched a self-hosted version of IBM Bob, its AI coding agent, letting enterprises run it inside their own secure environments — a direct response to agentic-AI security concerns. DigitalOcean introduced Managed Agents: managed cloud infrastructure for AI agents with isolated runtimes and governed tool access. Photon AI agents raised $4.5M in seed to replace traditional mobile-app interactions with conversational agents across messaging platforms.

Together they mark three deployment bets at once — on-prem for regulated coding, managed cloud for builders, and messaging-native for consumers. The through-line is trust over speed: agents that run for extended periods are hitting governance, not capability, as the bottleneck. The Photon figure and product claims come from a directory brief aggregating company announcements.

Key people & companies: IBM (IBM Bob); DigitalOcean (Managed Agents); Photon AI.

OpenAI Marketplace goes B2B: Decagon is a launch partner

Decagon announced it is a launch partner for OpenAI Marketplace, OpenAI's new B2B marketplace, under which eligible OpenAI enterprise customers can apply part of their existing OpenAI commitment toward Decagon's customer-facing AI agents. The mechanism reduces procurement friction by letting customers spend an existing commitment rather than signing a new vendor agreement — frontier labs are building app-store economics on top of committed enterprise spend, and agent vendors are competing for shelf space inside that channel. AI lawsuits surge across safety, copyright, and liability

#3 · States & cities · Connecticut · New Mexico · NYCOctober 1–5

State frontier-AI patchwork hardens: Connecticut in force, New Mexico drafts, NYC hearing Oct 5

While the federal White House accord (Sept. 29) remains voluntary — no penalties, disclosure duty, or deadline — binding state regimes are going live. Connecticut's Act Concerning Online Safety sections entered force October 1, imposing design and risk-management duties on frontier developers (training runs above 10^26 FLOPs) and subscription-transparency rules. New Mexico AG Raúl Torrez is preparing a 2027 bill requiring safety disclosure, incident reporting, advance notice of major training runs, and independent audits, with civil penalties. Separately, NYC Council's October 5 hearing will question OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, and Meta — attendance secured only after subpoena threats, with SpaceXAI subpoenaed after not responding. The pattern is a state-and-city compliance layer forming underneath a voluntary federal framework.

Key people & companies: Connecticut General Assembly; NM AG Raúl Torrez; Rep. Linda Serrato; NYC Council Speaker Julie Menin; OpenAI; Google; Anthropic; Meta; SpaceXAI.
