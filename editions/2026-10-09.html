#2 · Anthropic · Claude HaikuOctober 8

Anthropic ships Claude Haiku 5.5: 75% cheaper, with a price cliff at 100K tokens

New Haiku pricing is $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output — but both rates jump fivefold past a 100,000-token prompt, to $0.50 and $2.50. It’s the first Haiku-class model with an adjustable effort setting; Sonnet 5.5 cache reads were halved to $0.10/M.

The day’s biggest Hacker News thread spent itself on that cliff, called absurdly low for agent work, applying to Haiku but not Sonnet or Opus.

Key company: Anthropic.

02 / AGENTS & PROTOCOLS

Agents go to work: Google’s Gemini agent and a protocol war for commerce

#1 · Google Cloud · Work agentOctober 8Single-source

Google Cloud launches the Gemini agent, a single AI agent for work

The agent plans work, uses tools, connects to business systems and returns finished work in Docs, email and developer environments. It works inside Google Workspace and also Microsoft 365 and Slack, picks the best model per task — Gemini now, Claude models too, more later — and supports “coworker agents” with their own email addresses and scoped access.

Financial-services and legal versions are in preview; government, healthcare and retail are coming.

Key company: Google / Alphabet.

Reuters ↗
#2 · Sierra + Meta · Commerce protocolsOctober 6 proposalSingle-source · Vendor claims for protocol details

Sierra and Meta publish the Personal Agent Protocol; Visa’s rival already has Stripe and Shopify too

The Oct. 6 proposal from Sierra’s Bret Taylor and Clay Bavor and Meta is an OAuth-based open standard for how personal agents authenticate with businesses — guest access for stock checks, read-only or write access after customer sign-in, and sessions carried across channels, with v0.1 due later in October.

Partners include Walmart, Stripe, Shopify, Genesys, Instinct and Rocket — but payments aren’t in the first spec, and OpenAI and Anthropic aren’t on board. Visa’s Trusted Agent Protocol, introduced in June, already serves roughly 175 million merchants, with Stripe and Shopify in both camps.

Key people and companies: Sierra, Meta, Bret Taylor, Clay Bavor, Walmart, Stripe, Shopify, Genesys, Instinct, Rocket, Visa.

04 / RESEARCH & SCIENCE

Science in the crossfire: 722 machine-written math papers

#1 · OpenAI · MathematicsOctober 7Single-source · Vendor claims

OpenAI releases 722 manuscripts covering 372 topics from an unreleased internal math model

OpenAI dropped 722 manuscripts across algebra, number theory, theoretical computer science, logic and topology, generated by an internal frontier model it isn’t releasing, claiming progress on the Riemann hypothesis and the kakeya conjecture. Three papers were retracted within a day; only some proofs were certified by the Lean automated-verification platform, and OpenAI’s own researchers hadn’t read through them all.

The release follows the Sept. 11 declaration by 28 Fields Medalists warning against treating unsolved problems as benchmarks.

Key company: OpenAI.

Research notes: AECP multi-agent protocol, Recurrent Recurrent Transformer, EngramEdit knowledge editing

AWS AI Labs’ Artifact-Exclusive Communication Protocol forces multi-agent collaboration through structured artifacts, reporting a 28.2% increase in test pass rates and a drop in prompt-injection lateral movement from 95% to 0%. 05 / POLICY & REGULATION

Policy and physical limits

#1 · Denmark · DeepfakesOctober 8Single-source

Denmark moves to ban non-consensual AI likeness copies

Culture Minister Zenia Stampe will propose a bill banning digital copying and sharing of lifelike representations of a person — appearance and voice — to crack down on deepfakes, pushing tech giants to take manipulated content down fast. Caricature, parody and satire are excepted.

Parliamentary approval is still needed, with a hoped-for entry into force around the turn of the year.

Key person: Zenia Stampe.
