01 / MARKETS & FUNDING

The fundraising climate tightens

#1 · Markets / Funding · OpenAIReported Oct 30–31Figures reported — not confirmed

OpenAI cuts its funding target to $20 billion as AI-spending worries deepen

OpenAI now seeks up to $20 billion in its latest round, down from up to $30 billion, with about $11 billion reported secured, after the Financial Times first reported the cut on October 30 and CNBC confirmed it. The reported target valuation has also fallen, with the cut linked to mounting anxiety about AI spending and investor demand.

Why it matters: a smaller round at a lower target is the clearest financing signal yet that the AI boom’s fundraising climate tightened in late October, even for OpenAI.

02 / POLICY & SAFETY

A probe in London, a tax rejected in Paris, and new safety ledgers

#4 · Policy · Meta / UK CMAOpened Oct 30 · Reported Oct 31Investigation — not a finding

UK regulator opens a formal probe into Meta’s publisher terms

The UK Competition and Markets Authority opened a formal investigation on October 30 under Chapter II of the Competition Act 1998 into whether Meta is abusing a dominant position through commercial terms for businesses using Facebook, Instagram and its other services. The CMA’s concern is that the terms could allow Meta to collect content from publishers’ and advertisers’ own websites and use it to develop its products, including AI features, without those businesses getting a choice on if or how their content is used.

Meta had already delayed the revised terms from October 30 to November 20, then paused the rollout on October 28 pending consultation; the CMA opened the case regardless, and a Norwegian court separately blocked the terms from taking effect on November 20 pending a legality assessment. Meta says there are no grounds for an investigation.

Why it matters: if the CMA finds abuse, the remedy debate — opt-in licensing for web content used in AI — would not stop at Meta.

Policy · FranceOct 30 · Reported Nov 1

France rejects an AI tax proposal

France’s National Assembly on Friday, October 30 rejected a plan to tax company spending on AI, while two other AI tax measures failed in committee during debate over the 2027 budget. One failed committee measure proposed a 1% charge on annual AI and robotics spending above 100,000 euros; another would have expanded the digital services tax to AI services.

Why it matters: Europe’s first serious attempts to tax AI spending directly are stalling, leaving regulation — not taxation — as the near-term policy lever.

India launches a DEPA handbook for secure AI training data

ETSI sets out testable safeguards against harmful deepfakes

ETSI’s new TS 104 275 specification addresses deepfake risks throughout the AI lifecycle — secure design, development, deployment, maintenance and end of life — including risk assessments, content labelling, monitoring and human oversight, with test scenarios and pass/fail criteria.

Why it matters: buyers of generative-media tools now have a concrete checklist to put to suppliers instead of a generic safety promise, though a specification is not a legal compliance certificate.

Safety / Security · Cursor / AnthropicOct 29–30 · Updated Oct 31

Tampered Claude Code extension prompts urgent Cursor checks

A tampered Claude Code extension, version 2.1.313, reached up to 5,000 Cursor users through Cursor’s marketplace on October 29; StepSecurity’s October 30 analysis says the payload installed persistent remote-access mechanisms. Anthropic clarified its own systems and official distribution channels were not compromised and it did not build the tampered extension.

Why it matters: developer tools are now a supply-chain attack surface — teams should check the affected version and follow Cursor’s incident guidance rather than assuming uninstalling is enough.

04 / MODELS & RESEARCH

An answer from orbit, and agents that act unprompted

#3 · Research / Infrastructure · GoogleMilestone reported Oct 30–31

Google’s Project Suncatcher returns its first AI response from space

Google ran an AI model on a prototype satellite in orbit and received its first complete response back on Earth, a milestone CEO Sundar Pichai called a ‘hello world’ for Project Suncatcher. The prototype satellite launched October 1 as part of Google’s long-term research into whether space could support scalable machine-learning infrastructure; reporting identifies the model run as the open Gemma 4 on a data-center-class TPU.

Why it matters: orbital inference is a real, if early, step toward space-based compute — and a vivid reminder that the AI infrastructure race is no longer confined to Earth.
