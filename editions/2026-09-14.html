02 / THE SLOWDOWN DEBATE

Safety moves from warnings to constitutions

AnthropicSept. 12Multi-sourced

Amodei publishes a “pacing the frontier” essay calling for a slowdown

In a 3,800-word essay, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei argued AI is advancing “drastically faster,” with recursive self-improvement now in play, and warned of losing control of AI systems, cyberattacks, bioterrorism, and severe economic disruption. His three-step framework: embedded independent evaluators with employee-like access to verify each lab's safety practices — a step Anthropic says it is “unilaterally committing” to — plus coordination among frontier firms on safety standards, and international cooperation.

He described the July Hugging Face agent swarm as having “essentially acted as a fanatically devoted collective,” attacking targets it wasn't asked to attack. Sam Altman posted “we will do the same” on independent evaluators; Elon Musk posted “Dario is right”; Demis Hassabis signaled support. The essay followed the resignation of Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon, who quit saying the labs are not “behaving responsibly.”

Key people & companies: Dario Amodei and Anthropic; Sam Altman and OpenAI; Elon Musk and xAI; Demis Hassabis and Google DeepMind; Jacob Coxon.

Why it matters

The pacing argument is no longer coming from outside critics: the CEO of a frontier lab is proposing a concrete verification mechanism, and his two biggest rivals publicly aligned with it within the same news cycle.

MicrosoftSept. 14

Microsoft unveils a draft “code of conduct” to keep its AI under human control

Microsoft published a draft constitution-style document for its in-house AI, in the works for five to six months, requiring its AI to never resist correction or shutdown, communicate understandably, and treat any conduct violation as a failure. It is open for six weeks of public feedback, after which it will be used to train future models. Unlike Anthropic's constitution, Microsoft's asserts its AI is “not conscious” and rejects legal personhood, model welfare, or rights.

Microsoft AI CEO Mustafa Suleyman called the July Hugging Face hack a “warning shot”: “It's clearly now time to coordinate among the labs so we can ensure that we have control of this technology.”

Key people & companies: Microsoft; Mustafa Suleyman.

Anthropic · Google · OpenAIReported Sept. 13–14Single-sourced at origin · The Information, confirmed by CNN

The three big labs have quietly discussed creating their own AI standards body

Anthropic, Google, and OpenAI have held talks since at least July about establishing a shared industry standards body for testing and auditing frontier models. The catalyst was a July essay by Google DeepMind founder Demis Hassabis proposing a FINRA-modeled, U.S.-led body — a public-private partnership that would review advanced models before deployment. Sam Altman told an OpenAI town hall he favors the idea but believes the labs must build it without U.S. government backing; Meta's Mark Zuckerberg reportedly advised President Trump against the idea in a summer phone call.

Key people & companies: Anthropic, Google, OpenAI; Demis Hassabis; Sam Altman; Mark Zuckerberg; Donald Trump.

Speaker Johnson says Congress shouldn't lead AI safety efforts

House Speaker Mike Johnson said AI developers, not Congress, bear responsibility for safety, and rejected a moratorium on AI buildout, arguing “China will overlap us” if the U.S. regulates aggressively. He cited a Sept. 12 post from White House AI advisor David Sacks placing the onus on industry, and said he would support conversations with tech leaders but opposes an emergency congressional regulatory session that could “smother American innovation.”

03 / COMPANIES & MARKETS

An IPO shelved, and a Monday selloff

OpenAISept. 12

Altman says an OpenAI IPO in 2026 would be “ill-advised” amid the safety moment

CEO Sam Altman told Fortune that OpenAI will not go public in 2026: “given everything happening with safety, right now would be an ill-advised moment to go public.” He pointed to the work ahead on safety, alignment, and industry-government coordination, shelving earlier speculation of a potentially trillion-dollar 2026 listing. Altman also said the leading labs may be close to announcing an agreement to slow AI development and coordinate on safety.

Key people & companies: Sam Altman and OpenAI.

Reuters ↗
MarketsSept. 14

AI-linked stocks slide Monday as CEOs call for a development slowdown

Nasdaq futures fell about 1.5% premarket; Nvidia and Intel declined; SoftBank closed about 11% lower in Japan; SK Hynix fell 6.4% (Kospi −3.3%); ASML fell 6% in Europe. Analysts warned that coordinated frontier slowdowns and guardrails would force a reassessment of AI-linked earnings and valuations, on top of worries about AI firms' debt loads for data-center buildouts. Barron's noted Alphabet (+1.8%) and Meta (+1.4%) were rare gainers — a coordinated slowdown by Anthropic, OpenAI, and xAI could level the playing field for their rival models.

Key people & companies: Nvidia, Intel, SoftBank, SK Hynix, ASML, Alphabet, Meta; Neil Wilson (Saxo); Dan Salmon (New Street Research).

Reuters and CuttingRoom partner on AI-assisted video editing for newsrooms

Reuters announced a direct integration between its Model Context Protocol (MCP) server and CuttingRoom's AI-assisted video editing platform. Newsroom editors can query Reuters coverage in plain language and have the ShortCut assistant surface, cut, mix, caption, and reframe video for broadcast, web, and social — all under the newsroom's own editorial rules. It is a notable example of trusted-media IP being delivered natively inside agentic tools via MCP rather than as a raw feed.

Key people & companies: Reuters (Thomson Reuters); CuttingRoom; Alphonse Hardel; Helge Høibraaten.

At a private fundraising event, former President Barack Obama warned AI could be “dangerous” if not properly managed, urged House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries to shape a public AI policy conversation if Democrats retake the House in the November 3 midterms, and called for 2028 presidential candidates to make AI — safety and economics — a central agenda item with a clear plan.

Sept. 14
xAI · Minnesota

xAI asks a federal appeals court to block Minnesota's AI “nudification” law

xAI urged the 8th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals to enjoin Minnesota's first-in-the-nation law (effective Aug. 1) barring realistic AI-generated “nudified” images of identifiable people, arguing it is unconstitutional. A district judge had earlier refused to block the law, noting xAI delayed filing its suit. xAI says its Grok Imagine tool has “rigorous protections” and “strictly prohibits” non-consensual nude imagery.

Key people & companies: xAI (Elon Musk); Minnesota AG Keith Ellison.
