01 / MODELS & PLATFORMS

Agent standards get a neutral home, Google builds a computer per agent, and diagnosis gets a benchmark

#5 · Platforms · Agentic AI Foundation / Linux FoundationOct 22Adoption figures are Anthropic-reported — not independently verified

Four rival labs hand agent standards to a neutral foundation

At AGNTCon + MCPCon North America in San Jose on Oct 22, the Agentic AI Foundation (AAIF), under the Linux Foundation, announced four new projects: Agent Skills contributed by Anthropic, Open Responses by OpenAI, OpenEnv by Hugging Face, and OpenSandbox by Alibaba — taking AAIF from three launch projects in December 2025 to 10 hosted projects.

Anthropic says more than 70 agent platforms (including ChatGPT, Cursor, GitHub Copilot and VS Code) have integrated Agent Skills since December 2025, with 1 million skills published and nearly 280 million installed — those adoption figures are Anthropic-reported, not independently verified. Why it matters: shared, vendor-neutral standards for skills, model interoperability, training environments and safe execution reduce lock-in as agents become the industry’s main battleground.

#6 · Platforms · Google CloudOct 2210x density and benchmark figures are Google’s claims — not independently validated

Google Cloud’s Agent Compute Engine gives every agent its own isolated microVM

Announced Oct 22 at AGNTCon + MCPCon, Agent Compute Engine runs each AI agent inside a hardware-isolated microVM with its own guest kernel and root access, secured by Google’s Titanium IPU; credentials are kept outside the sandbox and injected at network egress, so a prompt-injected agent finds an empty sandbox. Idle agents are checkpointed and paused; copy-on-write memory sharing is claimed to deliver about 10x density — that figure is Google’s claim, not independently validated.

It already runs Gemini’s agent workloads and Antigravity internally; customer preview is slated for “the coming months” with no pricing announced. Google also open-sourced Agent Substrate, a Kubernetes runtime on the same architecture. Why it matters: the major clouds are converging on microVM isolation — not containers — as the safe primitive for agents that execute untrusted code.

#3 · Research / Models · Microsoft AIOct 22Peer-reviewed, but text-only historical cases — not a clinical trial

Microsoft’s MAI-DxO hits 83.2% on complex diagnoses in Science — vs 36.1% for generalist doctors

A peer-reviewed study in Science (Oct 22), “Sequential diagnosis with language models,” introduces MAI-DxO (Microsoft AI Diagnostic Orchestrator) and the SDBench benchmark: instead of a one-shot answer, the system works a case step by step — asking questions, ordering tests, revising its diagnosis — using a panel of virtual physician agents. On 304 complex New England Journal of Medicine cases it reached 83.2% accuracy (85.5% in peak configurations) vs 36.1% for generalist physicians with reference access and about 22% unassisted; on 325 emergency-department cases (MIMIC-IV-ED) it scored 80.3% vs 46.8%.

Microsoft AI CEO Mustafa Suleyman frames it as a step toward “medical superintelligence.” Caveats, stated clearly: text-only historical cases, generalists not specialists, no physical exams or imaging, not a clinical trial, and MAI-DxO is not treating patients; a prospective study with Beth Israel Deaconess is planned and SDBench is being open-sourced.

#8 · Models · OpenAI Decisions APIOct 22Single-sourced — Vector Wire only at publication time

OpenAI makes its Decisions API generally available at $0.10 per million input tokens

Per Vector Wire, OpenAI made the Decisions API generally available on Oct 22, priced at $0.10 per million input tokens with no charges for output tokens or cache reads/writes. It runs on ChatGPT-Luna reconfigured for fast inference and returns typed outputs — predicates, choices among predefined options, and rubric scores — rather than prose, with up to 400 questions per request and claimed latency around 100–180 milliseconds (OpenAI API product lead Nikunj Handa).

Trial customers named include Box, Datadog, Ramp and Speak; GA added GPT-Live integration, prompt caching, and image/JSON inputs. It competes directly with TypeSafe’s Jev in the fast structured “decision model” category. All figures are as reported by that single source.

03 / SAFETY, SECURITY & POLICY

AI found the bugs, AI is working the exploits, and Washington redraws the investment map

#2 · Security · php2shellOct 21–22

AI-discovered PHP flaws force government websites offline across six countries

Two critical PHP vulnerabilities, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619 (both CVSS 9.2), patched Oct 21 in PHP 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 and 8.2.35, triggered precautionary shutdowns on Oct 22: the Dutch NCSC site, DigiD and MijnOverheid, Finland’s government services, French Interior Ministry local sites, Denmark’s national school platform, German federal IT sites, and in the UK the Metropolitan Police plus Edinburgh and Oxford councils. Unit 42 counts more than 22.6 million exposed internet-facing systems; Cloudflare called its emergency firewall rules a “STOPGAP only.”

OpenAI says its GPT-5.6 Sol helped security firm Calif identify one flaw, Wiz independently found both, and OpenAI calls the gap between AI-assisted discovery and disclosure the “Defender’s Window” — a window that in this case closed early when the flaws leaked ahead of embargo. Why it matters: AI has compressed both vulnerability discovery and weaponization timelines, and disclosure processes have not caught up.

#4 · Policy · US Treasury / COINS ActOct 22

Treasury proposes barring US investment in any Chinese AI developer

On Oct 22 the US Treasury issued a notice of proposed rulemaking implementing the COINS Act (passed December 2025 as part of the FY2026 defense bill), expanding the Outbound Investment Security Program to six fields: semiconductors, AI (termed “superintelligence”/SI in the proposal), quantum, high-performance computing, hypersonics, and — newly added — certain biotechnologies.

For AI, the proposal drops any compute threshold: development of any SI system by an entity in a country of concern (China including Hong Kong and Macau, Russia, Iran, North Korea, Cuba) would be a prohibited transaction, including agentic harnesses built on third-party models; purely internal use of an off-the-shelf system is carved out. Comments are due December 7, 2026; existing rules stay in force until final regulations take effect. Why it matters: Washington is moving from policing only the most powerful Chinese models to treating the whole private AI sector as off-limits for US capital.

#7 · Security · ARTEX / JapanReported Oct 19–22

Autonomous AI pentesting tool ARTEX used against about 20 Japanese companies

GMO Cybersecurity by IERAE reported that intrusions against roughly 20 Japanese companies used ARTEX, an open-source agentic penetration-testing tool developed in China that runs intrusions largely autonomously; eight confirmed targets had published breach notices in September or October, and IERAE found six more AI-driven attack servers aimed only at Japan. Attackers rotated several hundred IP addresses just below rate-limit thresholds to evade web application firewalls.

CrowdStrike had reported Oct 7 that ARTEX, relying mainly on a DeepSeek model, was used against South Korean financial organizations in late September–early October. Why it matters: a legitimate red-team tool is now documented in real cross-border intrusion campaigns.

DeepSeek reportedly wraps a ~$15B raise days after US restrictions

This round is reported, not confirmed — DeepSeek and the named investors did not comment, and sources say the lineup and details could still change.

Bloomberg first reported, and Reuters confirmed via four people familiar with the matter, that Chinese lab DeepSeek is wrapping up a funding round of about 100 billion yuan (~$15 billion) — roughly double its original 50 billion yuan target — at a reported pre-money valuation of 500 billion yuan.
