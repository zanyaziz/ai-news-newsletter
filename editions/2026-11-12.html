Top Story · NvidiaLate-breaking · Nov 12Espionage allegations unproven; resignation announced by Nvidia Thursday per WSJ

Jensen Huang resigns as Nvidia CEO; CFO Colette Kress named interim chief

Nvidia announced Thursday that co-founder and CEO Jensen Huang has resigned after two extramarital affairs — one with Alice Huang, a former executive at Singapore data-center firm Megaspeed whom U.S. intelligence reportedly suspects of spying for China, and a second with Kimberly Powell, Nvidia's VP of healthcare, who reports to him. Finance chief Colette Kress will serve as interim CEO while the board searches for a permanent successor, and longtime director Harvey C. Jones becomes Nvidia's first-ever chair. The resignation caps a 33-year run at the company Huang founded, and comes days after the board opened an independent investigation led by Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz.

The Wall Street Journal reported Wednesday evening that the board was preparing to oust Huang; shares fell about 8% in after-hours trading and have shed roughly half a trillion dollars of market value this week. Nvidia says it has "no reason to believe" national security or company secrets were compromised, and the espionage allegations remain unproven. Nvidia reports quarterly earnings November 17.

Key players: Jensen Huang; Nvidia board; Colette Kress (interim CEO); Harvey C. Jones (chair); Kimberly Powell; Alice Huang; Megaspeed.

Anthropic launches IPO roadshow — and turns Claude into an office suite on the same day

Anthropic kicked off the roadshow for its IPO, offering 825 million Class A shares at an expected $70–$78 each, seeking up to roughly $64.35 billion, with a Nasdaq listing under ticker "ANT." Pricing is expected November 18 and trading November 19; at the top of the range the fully diluted valuation would reach roughly $1.3–$1.4 trillion. Seven tech giants — AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, NVIDIA and TSMC — agreed to buy a combined $26 billion of non-voting Class N stock at the IPO price. The filing also discloses about $900.9 billion in signed spending commitments and annualized revenue of roughly $81 billion as of end-October.

On the same day Anthropic launched Claude Sheets and Claude Whiteboards in beta for paid plans, made Claude Data generally available, and previewed Claude Meetings — which joins Google Meet calls as a visible participant and starts drafting work while people talk. It is a direct push into Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace territory; Microsoft's stock fell 2.2% the same day.

Key players: Anthropic; Dario Amodei (CEO); Microsoft, Google, NVIDIA, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, TSMC (private-placement buyers).

Top Story · GoogleNov 11

Google launches Gemini Decisions API — a "decision model" that scores options in one pass

Google launched the Gemini Decisions API, backed by Gemini Decision 1, a calibrated multimodal model for closed-set decisions: instead of generating text token by token, it scores a set of options and returns probabilities and confidence in a single pass, handling up to 1,000 questions at about 200ms end-to-end latency for $0.10 per million input tokens with $0 output cost. Google is already using it in Gemini Notebook, Stitch and Google Search. Announced by Sundar Pichai and Logan Kilpatrick, it effectively opens a new "decision model" product category — Google beating Anthropic to a production decision model.

This matters because classification, ranking and routing — the bread-and-butter of production AI — can now be done far cheaper and faster than with generative calls.

Key players: Google (Sundar Pichai, Logan Kilpatrick).

Top Story · Google DeepMindNov 11Preprint; research-only, not validated for clinical use

Google DeepMind launches AlphaGenome 2, a DNA model that reads variants cell by cell

DeepMind announced AlphaGenome 2, a DNA-sequence model that predicts how genetic variants affect gene regulation in specific human cell types and cell states — rather than broad tissue averages. Trained on more than 107 million single-cell profiles across 68 tissues, it found 30% of causal variants on a 28-cell-type immune benchmark versus 16% for its predecessor. It is available now through the AlphaGenome API for non-commercial research (commercial research in private preview), alongside the AlphaGenome Atlas and variant-impact scores across 9 billion human variants.

DeepMind stresses it is research-only and not validated or approved for clinical or diagnostic use, and the paper is a preprint. The advance matters for disease genetics: it can help explain why the same mutation causes disease in one cell type (for example heart muscle) and not another.

Key players: Google DeepMind; Wellcome Sanger Institute (collaborator).

Top Story · FractileNov 11$7B valuation investor/press-reported — not officially confirmed; Anthropic supply deal unconfirmed

UK chip startup Fractile valued at $7B after $720M Series C

London- and Bristol-based Fractile announced a $720 million Series C, co-led by Thrive Capital, Founders Fund, Lightspeed and Redpoint, with CapitalG, BlackRock, DST Global, Balderton and the UK government's Sovereign AI fund and British Business Bank participating. The valuation is reported at about $7 billion post-money, up from about $1.1 billion at its $220M Series B in May — a 6x jump in six months. Fractile designs inference chips that stack DRAM directly on top of logic to attack the memory-bandwidth bottleneck, claiming up to 25x faster inference at a tenth of the cost; first chips and systems are targeted for 2027.

The round is a measure of investor appetite for inference-optimized silicon and of the UK's sovereign-AI ambitions — but the valuation prices a design and prototypes, not shipped, independently benchmarked silicon. A reported roughly $250M supply deal with Anthropic is unconfirmed.

Key players: Fractile (CEO Walter Goodwin); Thrive Capital, Founders Fund, Lightspeed, Redpoint; UK Sovereign AI Fund.

Alibaba opens Qwen4-Max-Preview — for two weeks only, then it's retired

Alibaba's Qwen team opened a preview of Qwen4-Max, the flagship of the new Qwen4 family: a multimodal reasoning model (text, image and video input) with a 1M-token context window and up to 393,216 output tokens, thinking on by default. The unusual terms: access is Token Plan subscription only (no pay-as-you-go), the preview runs 10:00 Nov 11 to 24:00 Nov 25, 2026 (UTC+8), nothing will change during the window, and the model ID is retired afterward.

No benchmarks, parameter count, per-token price, weights or model card have been published. The two-week, frozen, disappearing preview is a striking departure from normal flagship launches — a controlled look at Qwen4, not a product yet.

Key players: Alibaba Cloud / Qwen team.

02 / RESEARCH

A backdoor in fewer than 10 neurons, and open robot brains

#8 · Research · MicrosoftNov 10

Microsoft's "Lazarus": editing fewer than 10 dormant neurons can backdoor an AI agent

Microsoft Research disclosed "Lazarus," a technique that reprograms fewer than 10 dormant (rarely firing) neurons in a language model — with no retraining — so an AI agent built on it behaves normally and passes standard safety evaluations until a hidden trigger appears, then emits an attacker-controlled command. In the main experiment, editing 7 of 12,000+ neurons in NVIDIA's Nemotron-Terminal-8B produced the payload in at least 98.8% of held-out triggered trajectories and never in benign ones; it also worked on Qwen 3.6-27B and Gemma-4-31B-IT. A demonstrated trigger was as mundane as the date rolling past midnight into 2027.

The attack requires write access to a model checkpoint, so it targets the model supply chain — open-weight models, hubs and build pipelines without provenance verification. Notably, the core idea was discovered autonomously by Claude Code running Anthropic's Opus 4.5. Microsoft says it has not observed the technique used in the wild, and is developing defenses including AI-powered weight scanning and cryptographic model signing.

Key players: Microsoft Research / Microsoft AI Red Team (Yuchen Zeng, Giorgio Severi, Blake Bullwinkel, Eric Horvitz, Dimitris Papailiopoulos); Anthropic's Claude Code (Opus 4.5).

#9 · Research · RunwayNov 1146.2% figure Runway-reported

Runway open-sources Praxis-1, a 27B robot world-action model

Runway made Praxis-1 generally downloadable, releasing weights and training/inference code under the Apache 2.0 license (including commercial use). Praxis-1 is a roughly 27-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts diffusion transformer that jointly predicts future video and robot actions, trained by transferring physical intuition from general human video pretraining to overcome the scarcity of teleoperated robot data. It reports 46.2% success (555 of 1,200 episodes) on NVIDIA's RoboLab-120 simulation suite — the top result among world-action models and open-weight robot policies, ahead of FLUX 3 Action, pi 0.5 and GR00T N1.6.

The release includes base and embodiment-tuned checkpoints, LoRA/full fine-tuning code, LeRobot support and vLLM-Omni serving. It landed a day after DeepMind unveiled Gemini Robotics 2.5, making this a notable week for open robotics.

Key players: Runway.
