#1 · Companies · AnthropicAnnounced Oct 18No governance or dollar figure published yet

Anthropic puts 5% of itself into a public benefit fund

Anthropic will allocate 5% of its shares to a new wholly-owned Public Benefit Fund, subject to stockholder approval, with the board able to add 2% for each 50% valuation increase above a baseline. Uses include nonprofit donations, direct payments to the public, and help for workers displaced by AI; with earlier founder and employee pledges, the company says roughly 20% of its shares would support public benefit.

No governance structure, spending mandate or dollar figure has been published yet. The announcement lands ahead of a widely expected IPO. Elon Musk responded on X recommending shares go directly to the public and saying he plans next year to give one SpaceX share to every child in America — a claim with no mechanism published.

#3 · Companies · Agent accessReporting Oct 16–18Delta’s wording is softer than a ban

The web is closing to third-party AI agents

Schwab CEO Rick Wurster said Oct 16 the firm blocks large AI agents tens of times a week, naming Meta’s Muse, and asked who is liable when a credentialled agent gets it wrong; read-only MCP access is planned, money movement is not. JPMorgan’s Jamie Dimon warned of rogue agents on the Oct 13 earnings call.

Beyond banking, The Verge reported Amazon has blocked Muse since September 20, Yelp allows only licensed (paid) agents, and Delta says it has no third-party agent partnership or integration. Four sectors, one month, same answer. Caveat: Delta’s wording is softer than a ban, and user reports about Cars.com and Amtrak are unverified. Shopify is the contrast case, opening storefronts to agents.

#5 · Companies · Character.AI / DisneyOct 18

Disney absorbs most of Character.AI out of bankruptcy

Character.AI filed for Chapter 11, and Disney agreed to pay $20 million for assets/a licence to its technology and offer jobs to at least 60 employees — roughly $60 million a year in compensation — most of whom accepted, per a court filing. The path was smoothed by former Character.AI CEO Karandeep Anand, now Disney’s first CTO.

The collapse was driven by burn rate plus mounting legal exposure, including a lawsuit tied to a teenager’s suicide and child-safety investigations — a cautionary tale for consumer AI companions aimed at young users.

#6 · Companies · Siemens / NvidiaOct 19 · Late-breakingSavings figures are company claims

Siemens and Nvidia show AI-driven factory automation in Erlangen

Siemens unveiled AI solutions built with Nvidia at its Erlangen electronics plant: a fully virtual production ramp-up cuts engineering effort for new printed-circuit-board assemblies by up to 85% and time-to-market by up to 40%, and agent-based production planning adapts schedules to shortages and demand for savings of up to 20%.

Board member Cedrik Neike said Siemens aims to raise factory automation from 30–40% today to 70–80%, is investing €500 million to expand Erlangen by 2030, and argued manufacturers need 7–9% annual productivity gains to stay competitive — framing industrial AI as how high-wage countries keep manufacturing jobs.

Tenstorrent raises $1.5B at a $6.2B valuation

#12 · Products · WorldLaunched Oct 18

World launches ID verification for AI agents

Sam Altman’s World made its first industry-specific push: World ID for banking and finance, plus AgentKit in beta, which lets a person delegate World ID Proofs to an AI agent so the agent can prove it acts for a verified human without revealing that person’s information.

World ID for Zoom also launched in the Zoom App Marketplace, using a one-time Orb verification plus a live selfie to confirm the right human is in a consequential meeting. The new World ID app is available in 150-plus countries. It is trust infrastructure aimed squarely at the same problem the banks raised this week.

#2 · Safety · OpenAI / Anthropic / DeepMindReported Oct 19Maintenance of joint safeguards undisclosed

OpenAI, Anthropic and Google DeepMind confirm weeks of joint safety coordination

The three rival labs confirmed they have spent several weeks coordinating on AI safety, jointly unveiling cyber-focused models, safeguards and access programs. It is a notable break from purely competitive posturing, focused on the dual-use cyber problem where shared safeguards do not require sharing model architecture.

It lands the same week as a royal-hosted AI safety summit in Scotland and reports of an authorized exercise in which Claude was used to breach OpenAI’s internal systems. Caveat: how the joint safeguards will be maintained, and whether this becomes recurring or stays a one-off, has not been disclosed.

#16 · Policy · GSAEffective Oct 19

GSA’s LLM contract clause is now live

From today, GSA solicitations and contracts for LLMs, chatbots and agentic systems where the model is a material feature and handles government data must carry clause 552.239-7001, ‘Basic Safeguarding of Data within Large Language Model Artificial Intelligence Systems.’

It bars training on government data, requires incident reporting within 72 hours, protects the government’s ability to export and migrate its data, requires 30 days’ notice before a model is replaced or materially changed, and replaces earlier ‘unbiased AI principles’ language with an obligation to use reasonable efforts to prioritise accuracy and objectivity.

#17 · Safety · Anthropic / METROct 19Voluntary — not a safety audit or release gate

Anthropic gives watchdog METR ‘employee-like’ access

Anthropic has opened its systems to METR, a nonprofit evaluator, to investigate its risk levels, incidents and practices. METR’s own proposal covers alignment, control (whether monitoring would catch serious harm in time), and whether automating AI research is outpacing safeguards.

#18 · Policy · Anthropic draft billOct 19 follow-upDraft — no sponsor, not introduced

Backlash builds over Anthropic’s draft safety bill thresholds

This remains a draft — no sponsor, NOT introduced in Congress.

Anthropic’s privately circulated draft bill would let the government block a frontier model’s release only if it poses a foreseeable, imminent risk of more than $1 billion in damage or more than 50 people killed or seriously injured, tied to bioweapons, cyberattack or loss of control. Critics argue the bar is set so high that serious sub-threshold harms would go unaddressed, and Mistral CEO Arthur Mensch accused US labs of using apocalyptic warnings for regulatory capture.
