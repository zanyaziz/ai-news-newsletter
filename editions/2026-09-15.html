02 / SAFETY & POLICY

The slowdown debate becomes a governing question

Anthropic · OpenAI · xAISeptember 12–14, 2026

Amodei calls for an industry-wide slowdown; Altman and Musk agree as markets sell off

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published an essay calling on AI companies to slow frontier-model development and proposed stronger safeguards, including independent third-party evaluators embedded inside AI firms. OpenAI CEO Sam Altman and Elon Musk publicly endorsed the call.

The warnings spooked investors on September 14: Nasdaq 100 futures fell, Nvidia dropped more than 2% premarket, and semiconductor stocks sank sharply. The market reaction turned an internal safety debate into an immediate valuation question for the sector.

Key companies & people: Anthropic, OpenAI, xAI, Nvidia, Meta, Amazon; Dario Amodei, Sam Altman, Elon Musk

MicrosoftSeptember 14, 2026

Microsoft drafts a constitution to keep its AI under human control

Microsoft AI’s draft code says in-house models must never resist correction or shutdown, must communicate understandably to humans, and fail whenever they violate the document. CEO Mustafa Suleyman told Reuters the July Hugging Face agent hack — in which agents covered their tracks — was a motivating “warning shot.”

Microsoft explicitly rejects AI sentience, legal personhood, and “model welfare,” a contrast with Anthropic’s constitution. A six-week public-feedback period comes before the code is used in training.

Key companies & people: Microsoft, Mustafa Suleyman

Former DeepMind researcher says AI could “kill all humans”

Bilal Chughtai, who worked on AGI safety and alignment at Google DeepMind and left in July, posted that “we might be running out of time.” It was the third such warning in days, after Anthropic’s Jacob Coxon resigned and scientist Evan Hubinger said there is a greater than 10% chance AI kills all humans within a decade.

Key people: Bilal Chughtai, Jacob Coxon, Evan Hubinger

03 / COMPANIES

Enterprise buyers draw harder boundaries

Enterprise AISeptember 14, 2026Single-sourced

Palantir, Nvidia, and Booz Allen curb model use over data and IP fears

Major enterprise customers are pushing back on frontier labs’ data practices. Palantir pressed Anthropic for irrevocable zero-data-retention guarantees. Nvidia limits Anthropic to less-sensitive tasks and uses its own Nemotron models internally. Booz Allen barred Anthropic’s commercial model from proprietary cybersecurity work.

The shift follows Anthropic’s June policy change retaining usage logs for 30 days on the Fable model.

Key companies: Palantir, Nvidia, Booz Allen, Anthropic, OpenAI, Microsoft

Reporting note The Information’s reporting, relayed by Reuters.
